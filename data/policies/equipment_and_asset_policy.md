# Equipment and Asset Policy (EQP-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy governs the equipment Northwind Systems Inc. provides to employees: what is
issued, when it is replaced, how it is looked after, and what happens to it when someone
leaves. It is the authoritative source for the boundary between equipment Northwind
supplies and costs an employee claims back.

**Who this policy covers.** All employees of both Northwind entities. Contractors use
equipment on the terms agreed in their statement of work; where a contractor is issued a
Northwind device, the security and return obligations in §5 and §6 apply to them in full.

### 1.1 The boundary with expenses

**Company-provided equipment is not an expense.** This is the most important sentence in
this policy and the one most often missed.

A laptop is issued by IT under this policy. It is never bought by an employee and claimed
back, at any price, with any approval. The same is true of the monitor provision in §3.
Peripherals that IT does not issue are a different matter and come out of the home office
stipend in EXP-01.

The test is simple: **if IT issues it, raise a request with IT. If it comes out of the
home office stipend, claim it under EXP-01.** An employee who buys company-provided
hardware personally is unlikely to be reimbursed and may end up owning a device that
cannot be used for Northwind work, because it cannot be enrolled and managed.

### 1.2 Definitions

**Asset.** Any item of equipment Northwind owns and issues to an individual. Assets are
recorded in the asset register against the person holding them.

**Refresh.** Scheduled replacement of a device at the end of its cycle, per §2.2.

**Early replacement.** Replacement before the cycle ends, per §2.3.

**Loaner.** A device issued temporarily, including the travel loaners required by SEC-01.

### 1.3 Related documents

- EXP-01 — expenses, including the home office stipend that covers peripherals and
  furniture, and the annual refresh amount.
- SEC-01 — data security and acceptable use, including device configuration, personal
  device rules, and the loaner requirement for high-risk travel.
- ONB-01 — onboarding, including equipment provisioning for new joiners.
- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance.

## 2. Laptops

### 2.1 Provision

Every employee is issued a laptop appropriate to their role, configured and managed by IT
under SEC-01. The laptop is Northwind property throughout, and it is the device on which
Northwind work is done.

New joiners are provisioned before their start date wherever possible, as part of the
onboarding sequence in ONB-01.

### 2.2 The refresh cycle

Laptops are refreshed on a **36-month cycle**.

IT tracks the cycle in the asset register and contacts employees as their device
approaches the date. An employee does not need to request a scheduled refresh, and does
not need to justify one: reaching 36 months is the justification.

A device that is still working well at the end of its cycle may be kept in service by
agreement with IT. The cycle is a maximum age, not a mandatory disposal date, and IT
makes the call based on whether the device still performs and can still receive security
updates.

### 2.3 Early replacement

Replacement before the end of the 36-month cycle requires **manager and IT approval,
together with documented failure**.

All three elements are required. The documented failure is the substantive one: a device
that crashes repeatedly, has failing hardware, cannot hold a charge, or can no longer run
the tools the role needs. "Documented" means IT has seen the problem or the diagnostic
logs, not that the employee has described it.

Preference is not failure. Wanting a newer model, a different operating system, or a
larger screen is not grounds for early replacement, and neither a manager nor IT can
approve on that basis.

An employee whose device is genuinely failing should raise it with IT promptly rather than
working around it for months. A failing device is a productivity problem and often a
security one, since a device that cannot update cannot be kept current.

### 2.4 Repairs

Where a device can be repaired within a sensible time, IT repairs it rather than
replacing it, and provides a loaner for the interval where the employee cannot work
without one. Repair does not restart the refresh cycle.

### 2.5 Laptops are not expensed

Stated again because it is the trap: a laptop is **never** an employee expense. It is not
claimable under the home office stipend in EXP-01, it is not claimable as a business
expense, and it is not claimable at any amount. An employee who needs a laptop raises a
request with IT.

## 3. Monitors and Peripherals

### 3.1 Monitors

Every employee is provided **one 27-inch monitor**. A **second monitor requires manager
approval**.

The first monitor needs no approval and no justification. The second is a manager
decision, and managers should approve it where the role genuinely benefits — anyone
working across several windows continuously — rather than treating it as a perk to be
rationed.

Monitors are issued by IT under this policy and, like laptops, are not expensed.

### 3.2 Peripherals

Keyboards, mice, headsets, cables, docks, laptop stands, and monitor arms are **not
issued under this policy**. They come out of the **home office stipend in EXP-01**.

This is the boundary in §1.1 applied in practice. IT issues the laptop and the monitor;
everything else on the desk comes from the stipend, subject to the stipend's own tenure
requirement and approval thresholds, which are defined in EXP-01.

### 3.3 Furniture

Desks, chairs, and lamps are furniture, not equipment. They are covered by the home
office stipend in EXP-01 and are not issued by IT. An employee asking IT for a chair
should be routed to EXP-01; an employee asking Finance for a laptop should be routed
here.

### 3.4 Accessibility equipment

An employee who needs specific equipment for a medical or accessibility reason should
raise it with People Operations rather than with IT or as an expense claim. Adjustments
of that kind are handled separately and are not limited by the monitor provision in §3.1
or by the stipend in EXP-01.

## 4. Office and Travel Equipment

### 4.1 Equipment at the office

Northwind offices provide docks, monitors, and meeting-room equipment at shared desks.
Employees do not need to carry peripherals between home and office for ordinary hybrid
working under REM-01.

### 4.2 Taking equipment to another location

Equipment issued for the home location may be taken elsewhere when working temporarily
from another location under REM-01. Northwind does not ship bulky items — monitors, docks
— for temporary arrangements.

### 4.3 Travel loaners

Travel to a country Security classifies as high-risk requires a loaner device issued by
the Security team, under SEC-01. The employee's usual laptop does not travel. Because
issuing a loaner takes time, employees should raise such travel as early as REM-01's
notice period allows.

### 4.4 Loaner returns

Loaners are returned promptly on return from travel or at the end of a repair. A loaner
held indefinitely is an asset Northwind cannot account for, and IT will chase it.

## 5. Care, Loss, and Damage

### 5.1 Looking after equipment

Employees are expected to take reasonable care of Northwind equipment: keep it out of
extreme conditions, avoid liquids near it, use a case in transit, and never leave it
unattended in public or in a vehicle. Devices must not be checked into hold luggage.

Northwind does not charge employees for ordinary wear, accidental damage, or the failures
that happen to hardware in normal use. Employees should report damage rather than
concealing it; a damaged device is often a security risk as well as a broken one.

### 5.2 Lost or stolen equipment

**Lost or stolen equipment must be reported within 24 hours, and is treated as a security
incident.**

Two things follow from the second half of that sentence. The report goes to both IT and
Security. And the incident timeline in SEC-01 applies as well: SEC-01 requires incidents
to be reported within 2 hours of discovery, which is the tighter of the two windows and
is the one to work to for a device that held Northwind data.

The practical instruction is therefore: report immediately, to Security and to IT. Do not
wait to search for the device first, and do not wait for business hours. IT can lock and
wipe a device remotely, and that option is worth most in the first hour.

### 5.3 Recovered equipment

A device that turns up after being reported must not simply be put back into use. Hand it
to IT, which will assess whether it can be trusted and will reissue or retire it.

### 5.4 Repeated loss

A single loss is bad luck. A pattern is a conversation between the employee, their
manager, and Security about how equipment is being handled, and in serious cases a
conduct matter under CON-01.

## 6. Return on Termination

Equipment must be returned **within 5 business days** of the last day of employment.

### 6.1 What must be returned

Everything Northwind issued: the laptop and its charger, monitors, docks, hardware keys,
loaners, access cards, and any other item recorded against the employee in the asset
register.

IT provides return instructions and packaging during the offboarding process, including
for employees who are not near an office.

### 6.2 Data

Employees must not retain copies of Northwind data of any classification, including work
they personally produced, per SEC-01 §6.5. Anything personal on the device should be
removed before return; IT will not retrieve personal files from a returned device and
devices are wiped on receipt.

### 6.3 Equipment not returned

Equipment still outstanding after 5 business days is followed up by IT. Devices that
cannot be recovered are remotely wiped and their access revoked. Persistent failure to
return company property is handled as a conduct and, where appropriate, a legal matter
rather than an IT one.

### 6.4 Access removal

Access is removed on the last day of employment, per SEC-01 §6.5. This happens
independently of equipment return: an employee who has not yet returned a device has no
access on it, and a returned device is not a substitute for access removal.

### 6.5 Transfers between entities

An employee transferring between the US and Irish entities does not return equipment
unless IT says otherwise; the asset record moves with them. A transfer is not a
termination for the purposes of this section.

## 7. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 7.1 Employees

Employees take reasonable care of equipment, report loss or theft immediately and within
24 hours at the latest, report damage rather than concealing it, return loaners promptly,
raise genuine device failures with IT rather than working around them, and return
everything within 5 business days of leaving.

### 7.2 Managers

Managers approve second monitors under §3.1 and, with IT, early replacements under §2.3.
They should approve a second monitor where the role benefits, decline early replacement
requests based on preference rather than documented failure, and make sure leavers'
equipment is returned.

### 7.3 IT

IT owns this policy, provisions and configures devices, maintains the asset register and
the refresh cycle, assesses documented failures, repairs and issues loaners, manages
returns, and removes access on the last day of employment.

### 7.4 Security

Security classifies high-risk destinations, issues travel loaners, and handles the
incident side of any lost or stolen device under SEC-01.

### 7.5 Out of scope

This policy covers Northwind-issued equipment. It does not cover peripherals or furniture,
which come from the home office stipend in EXP-01, and it does not cover data-handling
rules, which are in SEC-01. Where an employee asks about equipment or an allowance not
described here or in another Northwind policy, the correct answer is that the policy
library does not cover it and the question should go to IT.
