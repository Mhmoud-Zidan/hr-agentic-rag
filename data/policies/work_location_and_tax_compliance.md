# Work Location and Tax Compliance (TAX-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy explains the tax and registration consequences of where Northwind Systems
Inc. employees work, and sets out what employees must report and when. It is the
authoritative source for the thresholds that trigger a tax or registration obligation
for Northwind.

This policy answers **what follows** from a working location. It does not decide whether
an employee may work from a location — that question is answered by REM-01. The two
documents work together: REM-01 sets the duration tiers and approval requirements, and
several of those tiers require a review conducted against the thresholds in this policy.

**Who this policy covers.** All employees of Northwind Systems Inc. The reporting
obligation in §5 applies to every employee regardless of employment type, working
pattern, or seniority. Employees of Northwind Systems Ltd. (Dublin, Ireland) are subject
to Irish rules administered by the EMEA Finance team; the principles in §3 and §4 apply
to them in reverse when they work from the United States.

**Contractors.** Contractors are not employees and are outside this policy. A
contractor's tax position is their own and their vendor's.

### 1.1 What this policy is not

This policy is not personal tax advice, and nothing in it should be read as advice about
an individual's own tax position. It describes obligations that fall on **Northwind** as
an employer, and the reporting employees must do so that Northwind can meet them.
Employees with questions about their personal filing position should consult their own
tax adviser. Northwind cannot advise employees on their personal returns and will not
reimburse the cost of that advice.

### 1.2 Definitions

**Nexus.** A connection between Northwind and a US state sufficient to create a filing,
registration, or withholding obligation for Northwind in that state. Employee presence
is one of the things that creates it.

**Registered state.** A US state in which Northwind is already registered as an employer
and can therefore payroll an employee without creating a new obligation.

**Permanent establishment.** The equivalent concept for another country: a presence
substantial enough that Northwind may be treated as carrying on business there, with
corporate tax and filing consequences that follow.

**Cumulative days.** The total of all days spent working in a location across the
relevant period, whether or not they are consecutive. This is deliberately different
from the *consecutive* counting used for the approval tiers in REM-01 §4, and §3.2
explains why the difference matters.

**Calendar year.** January 1 to December 31, which is also Northwind's fiscal year.

### 1.3 Related documents

- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work. Whether a location is permitted at all, the duration
  tiers, the approved country list, and the approval routes.
- BEN-01 — benefits, including the separate Irish benefits schedule that applies on a
  transfer to the Irish entity.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance, including how to raise a concern.

## 2. Where Northwind Is Registered

Northwind Systems Inc. is registered as an employer in **Texas, California, New York,
Washington, and Colorado**.

An employee whose home location is in one of these five states can be payrolled normally,
and short periods of work in any of them raise no new registration question for
Northwind.

### 2.1 What registration means for an employee

Registration is about Northwind's obligations, not the employee's convenience. In a
registered state, Northwind can already withhold and report correctly. In a state where
Northwind is not registered, an employee working there beyond the threshold in §3 may
oblige Northwind to register, which has a cost and a lead time and cannot be arranged
retrospectively without difficulty.

### 2.2 The list is a snapshot

Northwind's registration footprint changes as the company grows. Employees should check
the current position with Finance rather than relying on what was true when they joined
or on what a colleague was permitted previously. A state that is not on the list today
may be added later, and a planned arrangement should be assessed against the position at
the time it is reviewed.

### 2.3 Offices and registration are different questions

Northwind has offices in Austin, San Francisco, and New York, and is registered in those
three states plus Washington and Colorado. The presence of an office is not what creates
registration, and the absence of an office does not mean an employee cannot work in a
state — Colorado and Washington are registered without an office in either.

## 3. The US State Nexus Threshold

Working in a US state where Northwind is **not** registered creates a nexus risk once an
employee reaches **30 cumulative days in that state in a calendar year**.

### 3.1 How the threshold works

The count is **cumulative across the calendar year**, not per trip. Every day worked in
the state counts toward the same total, and the total resets only at the start of the
next calendar year.

Reaching the threshold does not automatically create an obligation in every case — the
rules vary by state — but it is the point at which Northwind must assess the position.
That assessment is Finance's, not the employee's and not the manager's.

### 3.2 Cumulative days versus consecutive days

This is the distinction employees most often get wrong, and it is worth stating directly.

REM-01 §4 sets approval tiers by **consecutive** business days in a single block. This
policy counts **cumulative** days across the whole calendar year. The two counts are
different numbers measuring different things, and satisfying one says nothing about the
other.

The practical consequence: a series of short trips, each individually short enough to
need no approval under REM-01 §4.1, can still add up to the threshold in this section. An
employee who spends a week working from the same non-registered state every month has
made no single request requiring approval and has nonetheless created a position Finance
needs to assess. This is why REM-01 §4.4 asks employees and managers to look at repeated
trips as a pattern rather than in isolation.

### 3.3 What happens when the threshold is approached

Where Finance identifies that an employee is approaching or has passed the threshold in a
non-registered state, the possible outcomes are that Northwind registers in that state,
that the employee's working pattern is adjusted so the threshold is not exceeded, or that
the employee's home location is formally changed with the consequences that follow.

None of these outcomes is punitive. The employee is not in trouble for having travelled;
the obligation is Northwind's, and the only real failure mode is Finance not knowing in
time to deal with it.

### 3.4 Registered states

Time worked in a registered state does not count toward this threshold, because there is
no new registration to trigger. An employee whose home location is in a registered state
and who works from another registered state raises no question under this section,
however long the stay — though REM-01's approval tiers still apply to the arrangement.

## 4. International Presence and Permanent Establishment

Any international presence of **more than 30 days in a 12-month period** carries
permanent establishment risk.

### 4.1 Why this threshold is stricter than the domestic one

A permanent establishment exposes Northwind to corporate tax, filing obligations, and
potentially employment obligations in another country. The consequences are more serious
and harder to unwind than a state registration, and they can arise from one employee's
presence.

This is the tax reasoning behind the hard limit in REM-01 §5.3, which prohibits
international work beyond 30 calendar days outright rather than subjecting it to a
review. The prohibition exists precisely so that this threshold is not reached by
accident.

### 4.2 The 12-month window

The international count runs over any rolling 12-month period, not over the calendar
year. An employee cannot reset the count by waiting for January. Two separate approved
stays in the same country, in the same rolling year, are assessed together.

### 4.3 Approved countries do not mean unlimited time

Ireland, Canada, and the United Kingdom are the only countries in which Northwind permits
employees to work, per REM-01 §6. That permission is about where an approvable
arrangement can exist; it does not create an allowance of time. The threshold in this
section applies to those countries as it does to any other, which is why REM-01's
duration tiers cap international stays well below the point at which the risk arises.

An employee who has already spent time working in Ireland during the current rolling year
should say so when requesting another stay there, because the Tax review assesses the
total, not the new request alone.

### 4.4 Transfers to a local entity

Where an employee needs to work from another country for longer than REM-01 permits, the
only route is a transfer to a Northwind entity in that country, which requires an entity
to exist there. A transfer moves the employee onto that entity's payroll and benefits
schedule and removes the permanent establishment question, because the entity is already
established. The benefits consequences are described in BEN-01.

### 4.5 Tax equalization is not offered

Northwind does **not** offer tax equalization. An employee who works from another
location, domestically or internationally, bears their own personal tax consequences,
including any additional filing obligation or additional liability arising in that
location.

This is a deliberate policy position and it applies to every arrangement, including ones
Northwind has approved. An approval under REM-01 confirms that Northwind can accommodate
the arrangement; it is not a statement that the employee will be no worse off personally,
and it carries no undertaking to make them whole. Employees should establish their own
position before committing to an extended arrangement, and should factor the cost of
advice into that decision.

## 5. Employee Reporting Obligations

Employees must **report any location change of more than 5 business days via the HR
portal**.

### 5.1 What must be reported

The obligation covers working from anywhere other than the employee's home location for
more than 5 business days, whether domestic or international, and whether or not the
arrangement required approval under REM-01.

This threshold is deliberately lower than REM-01's no-approval tier. An arrangement can
require no approval and still require reporting. The reason is in §3.2: Finance cannot
assess a cumulative position it cannot see, and the individually unremarkable trips are
exactly the ones that accumulate unnoticed.

A permanent change of home location must also be reported, as soon as it is known rather
than after the move.

### 5.2 How and when to report

Reports are filed in the HR portal with the location and the start and end dates. A
report should be filed in advance where the arrangement is planned, and promptly where
circumstances changed at short notice.

Reporting is not the same as requesting. An international arrangement needs approval
under REM-01 §5 *and* a report under this section; filing one does not satisfy the other,
though the HR portal will usually capture both from a single submission where the
arrangement was requested through it.

### 5.3 Why this matters

Northwind's ability to meet its obligations depends entirely on knowing where its
employees are working. An unreported arrangement can create a registration or
establishment position that is discovered late, when the options for dealing with it are
worse and more expensive.

Employees are not expected to assess their own nexus position, work out whether a state
is registered, or judge whether a stay creates permanent establishment risk. They are
expected to report accurately and on time. The assessment is Finance's job and the
threshold questions are answered in §3 and §4.

### 5.4 If you realise you should have reported

An employee who realises they have not reported something they should have should report
it as soon as they notice. A late report is substantially better than no report, and
Northwind treats a voluntary late disclosure as a compliance success rather than a
failure. Deliberately concealing a working location is a different matter and is handled
under CON-01.

## 6. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 6.1 Employees

Employees report location changes of more than 5 business days through the HR portal,
report permanent changes of home location as soon as they are known, disclose prior time
already spent in a country when requesting another stay there, and obtain their own
advice on their personal tax position. Employees are not responsible for assessing nexus
or permanent establishment risk.

### 6.2 Managers

Managers are an approver on the REM-01 tiers that require them, and are expected to look
at repeated short trips to the same location as a pattern rather than as separate
requests. Managers should not attempt to assess a tax position themselves, and should
route anything that looks like a threshold question to Finance rather than approving
around it.

### 6.3 Finance and Tax

Finance owns this policy, maintains the registration footprint in §2, conducts the tax
reviews required by REM-01 §4.3 and §5.2, monitors cumulative positions against the
thresholds in §3 and §4, and decides whether Northwind registers in a new state.

### 6.4 People Operations

People Operations maintains location records in the HR portal, routes reports to Finance,
and handles permanent changes of home location and entity transfers.

### 6.5 Out of scope

This policy covers Northwind's tax and registration obligations arising from employee
work locations, and the reporting employees owe in support of them. It does not cover
personal tax advice, personal filing obligations, or the cost of obtaining advice, and it
does not offer tax equalization. Where an employee asks about a matter not described here
or in another Northwind policy, the correct answer is that the policy library does not
cover it and the question should go to Finance or People Operations.

### 6.6 Version and review

This is version v4.2, effective 2026-01-01. It supersedes all prior versions of the
Northwind Work Location and Tax Compliance policy. It is owned by Finance and Tax and is
reviewed annually, and the registration footprint in §2 is reviewed more frequently. A
consolidated view of every approval threshold that references this policy is maintained
in APR-01.
