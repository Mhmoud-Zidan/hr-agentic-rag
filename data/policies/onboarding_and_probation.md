# Onboarding and Probation (ONB-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy covers an employee's first months at Northwind Systems Inc.: how they are
onboarded, how the probation period works, and which entitlements are available during
it. It is the authoritative source for the length of the probation period and for the
probation review schedule.

Several other policies depend on the probation framework defined here and reference it
rather than restating it. This document owns the definition; those documents own their
own consequences.

**Who this policy covers.** All new employees of Northwind Systems Inc. Employees of
Northwind Systems Ltd. (Dublin, Ireland) follow this onboarding sequence, with Irish
statutory provisions administered by the EMEA People Operations team.

**Contractors.** Contractors are not employees, do not serve a probation period, and are
onboarded through the engaging vendor. The security onboarding in §2.4 applies to anyone
given access to Northwind systems, contractors included.

### 1.1 Definitions

**Start date.** The first day of employment. It is day 1 for every count in this policy
and for accrual in PTO-01.

**Probation period.** The initial period described in §3, during which both the employee
and Northwind assess the fit.

**Probation review.** A structured conversation between the employee and their manager at
the points in §3.2.

### 1.2 Related documents

- PTO-01 — paid time off, sick leave, and other leave, including how each is treated
  during probation.
- BEN-01 — benefits, including the waiting period that determines when coverage starts.
- EXP-01 — expenses, including the home office stipend and its tenure requirement.
- EQP-01 — equipment, including provisioning for new joiners.
- SEC-01 — data security, including security onboarding.
- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix.

## 2. Onboarding

### 2.1 Before the start date

People Operations issues the employment paperwork and confirms the start date, the
assigned office, and the working pattern. IT provisions a laptop under EQP-01 and, where
possible, has it ready before day 1. The manager prepares the first week and identifies
who the new joiner will work with most closely.

### 2.2 Week one

The first week covers the practical setup and orientation: systems access, an
introduction to the team, and a walk through the policy library. New joiners are not
expected to be productive in week one and managers should not plan as though they will be.

Managers should schedule the first week on in-office days where the employee is hybrid
under REM-01. The onboarding case is the clearest example of why the in-office expectation
exists.

### 2.3 Benefits and payroll setup

New joiners complete their benefits elections within the window People Operations
communicates during onboarding. This window is separate from open enrollment, and missing
it means waiting — the consequences are set out in BEN-01 §5.2, and managers should check
that elections have been completed rather than assuming.

Coverage does not begin on day 1. The waiting period is defined in BEN-01 §3, and new
joiners should read it before scheduling non-urgent medical appointments.

### 2.4 Security onboarding

Every new joiner completes security onboarding in their first week: MFA enrolment, the
password manager, device configuration, and the data classification scheme in SEC-01.
Anyone whose role involves the most sensitive data class is issued a hardware key at this
point under SEC-01 §7.2.

Security onboarding applies to contractors as well as employees, because it follows
access rather than employment status.

### 2.5 The first 30 days

The manager's job in the first month is to make the expectations explicit: what the role
is for, what good work looks like, who to ask, and what the employee should be able to do
independently by the end of probation. An employee who reaches their first review unclear
about any of these has been managed poorly, and the review should say so.

## 3. The Probation Period

The probation period is **90 days** from the start date.

### 3.1 What probation is for

Probation is a structured assessment period for both sides. Northwind assesses whether
the role and the person fit; the employee assesses the same thing. It is not a trial
during which an employee has fewer rights, and it is not a formality to be ignored until
day 89.

### 3.2 Probation reviews

There are three scheduled reviews: at **day 30, day 60, and day 90**.

Each is a documented conversation between the employee and their manager covering what is
going well, what needs to change, and what support the employee needs. The manager records
the outcome with People Operations.

The purpose of having three is that nothing in the day 90 review should be a surprise. A
concern that first appears at day 90 is a failure of the day 30 and day 60 reviews, and
managers are expected to raise problems when they see them rather than saving them for the
final conversation.

### 3.3 Outcomes at day 90

The probation period ends with one of three outcomes: confirmation, extension under §3.4,
or termination of employment.

Confirmation is the normal outcome and is recorded by People Operations. No action is
required from the employee.

### 3.4 Extension

Probation may be extended **once, by up to 30 additional days**.

The limits are both real: one extension only, and a maximum of 30 additional days. A
second extension is not available, and an extension longer than 30 days cannot be
approved. Where a manager reaches the end of an extended probation still unsure, the
answer is a decision rather than more time.

An extension requires a clear statement to the employee of what needs to change and how it
will be assessed. An extension granted without that is not a fair process, and People
Operations will ask for it before recording the extension.

### 3.5 Termination during probation

Where the outcome is termination, People Operations manages the process. An employee whose
employment ends during or at the end of probation has accrued paid time off, which is paid
out on the terms in PTO-01 — accrual begins on day 1 regardless of whether the days could
be taken.

### 3.6 Probation and conduct

Probation is about fit and performance. A conduct concern is handled under CON-01 at any
point, during probation or after it, and is not deferred to a probation review.

## 4. What Applies During Probation

This section exists because the most common new-joiner questions are about which
entitlements are live during the first 90 days. The answers differ by entitlement, and
each is owned by another document.

### 4.1 Benefits are active

**Benefits are active during probation**, subject to the waiting period in BEN-01 §3.
Probation has no effect on benefits eligibility. An employee inside probation whose
waiting period has completed is covered normally.

### 4.2 Paid time off accrues but cannot be taken

**Paid time off accrues from day 1 but cannot be taken until day 91.**

Both halves matter. The balance is real and is growing from the first day of employment;
it simply is not available to take yet. A new joiner looking at a positive balance in the
HR portal is not looking at an error.

The accrual rate, the notice periods, and the point at which the days become usable are
all defined in PTO-01. The relevant interaction is that the usability date is fixed at day
91 of employment and **does not move if probation is extended** under §3.4. An employee on
an extended probation can take paid time off from day 91 like anyone else.

### 4.3 Sick leave is not restricted

Sick leave is available from day 1 and is not restricted by probation. A new joiner who is
unwell uses sick leave in the normal way, on the terms in PTO-01. Managers should tell new
joiners this explicitly, because employees in a new job often work while unwell for fear of
how it looks.

Bereavement leave is likewise unaffected by probation.

### 4.4 The home office stipend has its own tenure requirement

The home office stipend in EXP-01 has a tenure requirement of its own, defined there. It is
not the probation period and it should not be explained as one, even though the two
happen to be close together — they are separate rules in separate documents with separate
purposes.

An employee too new to claim the stipend should be told which rule they have hit: the
stipend has a tenure requirement, and that is different from a claim being refused.

### 4.5 Equipment is provided from day 1

Equipment provision under EQP-01 is not affected by probation. A new joiner receives the
same laptop and monitor provision as anyone else, from the start.

### 4.6 Remote and hybrid work

The working-location rules in REM-01 apply from day 1, including the in-office expectation
and the approval tiers for working elsewhere. New joiners should be aware that the
international notice period in REM-01 applies to them as it does to everyone, so a trip
planned before joining may not be approvable on the timeline they assumed.

## 5. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 5.1 Employees

New joiners complete their benefits elections within the onboarding window, complete
security onboarding, raise questions early rather than accumulating them, and use the
probation reviews to say what support they need.

### 5.2 Managers

Managers prepare for the start date, make expectations explicit in the first month, hold
all three probation reviews and record them, raise concerns when they appear rather than
at day 90, check that new joiners have completed benefits elections, and state clearly
what must change if they seek an extension.

### 5.3 People Operations

People Operations owns this policy, issues employment paperwork, communicates the
onboarding benefits window, records probation outcomes and extensions, and manages
terminations during probation.

### 5.4 IT and Security

IT provisions equipment before the start date under EQP-01. Security runs security
onboarding and issues hardware keys where the role requires them under SEC-01.

### 5.5 Out of scope

This policy covers onboarding and the probation framework. It does not cover the
entitlements themselves — leave is in PTO-01, benefits in BEN-01, expenses in EXP-01,
equipment in EQP-01 — and it does not cover performance management after probation ends.
Where a question is not answered here or in another Northwind policy, the correct answer
is that the policy library does not cover it and the question should go to People
Operations.
