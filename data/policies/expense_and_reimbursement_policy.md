# Expense and Reimbursement Policy (EXP-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy governs what Northwind Systems Inc. reimburses, what it does not, what
approval an expense needs, and how and when reimbursement is paid. It applies to every
business expense an employee incurs personally and claims back, and to spending on a
Northwind corporate card.

**Who this policy covers.** All employees of Northwind Systems Inc., full-time and
part-time. Employees of Northwind Systems Ltd. (Dublin, Ireland) follow this policy for
approval thresholds and submission, with amounts administered in local currency by the
EMEA Finance team.

**Contractors.** Contractors do not submit expenses under this policy. Contractor costs
are governed by the statement of work and invoiced through the engaging vendor. A
contractor asking to be reimbursed should be routed to the engaging manager.

### 1.1 The three questions

Every expense raises three separate questions, and confusing them is the most common
cause of a rejected claim:

1. **Is it reimbursable at all?** Some things are never reimbursable regardless of amount
   or approval, and some things are provided by Northwind rather than expensed. See §2
   and §8.
2. **What approval does it need?** Set by amount, per §4.
3. **What evidence does it need?** Set by amount, per §3.

An expense must clear all three. A manager's approval does not make a non-reimbursable
item reimbursable, and a receipt does not substitute for an approval.

### 1.2 Definitions

**Expense date.** The date the cost was incurred, which is the date on the receipt — not
the date it was noticed, submitted, or approved. The submission deadline in §3 runs from
this date.

**Business expense.** A cost incurred wholly and necessarily in doing Northwind work.

**Incremental cost.** The additional cost created by a personal choice layered onto a
business trip. See §7.5.

**Per diem.** A fixed daily allowance for meals while travelling, in place of itemised
meal receipts. See §6.

### 1.3 Related documents

- EQP-01 — equipment and assets. Laptops, monitors, and other company-provided hardware
  are governed there and are **not** expenses. See §9.1.
- ONB-01 — onboarding and probation, which sets the tenure that the home office stipend
  in §5 depends on.
- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work, for where an employee may work from.
- TAX-01 — work location and tax compliance.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance, including the route for raising a concern
  about a rejected claim.

## 2. What Northwind Reimburses

Northwind reimburses costs incurred wholly and necessarily in doing its work. In
practice that means business travel and accommodation (§7), meals while travelling (§6),
the home office setup described in §5, and the ordinary running costs of doing a job —
software licences, professional subscriptions, conference fees, and client meeting costs
— where they have the approval §4 requires.

### 2.1 The test

The test is whether the cost exists **because of** the work. A meal eaten while
travelling for Northwind exists because of the trip; the same meal eaten at home does
not. A monitor stand bought for a home desk used for Northwind work is a work cost; a
television in the same room is not.

Employees who are unsure should ask their manager or Finance **before** incurring the
cost. Asking afterwards is possible but puts the employee at risk of carrying a cost
personally, and Finance cannot make a non-reimbursable item reimbursable by sympathising
with the circumstances.

### 2.2 Spending someone else's money

Employees are asked to spend Northwind's money as carefully as their own, and to choose
the reasonable option rather than the cheapest available or the best available. The caps
in this policy are limits, not targets: a hotel under the cap in §7.3 is not a saving to
be spent elsewhere, and a per diem is not an allowance to be used up.

### 2.3 Corporate cards

Where an employee holds a Northwind corporate card, business costs should go on it rather
than on a personal card. Card spending is still subject to every rule in this policy —
the same approval thresholds in §4, the same receipt requirements in §3, and the same
exclusions in §8. A charge on a corporate card is not pre-approved by virtue of having
gone through.

Personal spending on a corporate card should be reported to Finance and repaid promptly.
An occasional mistake is an administrative matter; a pattern is a conduct matter under
CON-01.

## 3. Submission Deadline and Receipts

### 3.1 The submission deadline

Expenses must be submitted **within 45 days of the expense date**.

A claim submitted after 45 days may be declined. The deadline exists so that Northwind's
accounts reflect costs in the period they were incurred, and so that an employee is not
carrying a business cost on a personal card for months.

Employees who are unable to submit within the deadline — extended leave, illness, or a
genuine administrative failure — should submit as soon as they can with an explanation.
Finance may accept a late claim in those circumstances. Employees should not assume this;
the deadline is the rule and the exception is discretionary.

### 3.2 Receipts

A receipt is required for any expense **above $25**.

An expense of $25 or less may be claimed without a receipt, with a description of what
it was and why. This exists so that small, receipt-less costs — a transit fare, a
coffee for a client — do not create more administrative work than they are worth.

A receipt must show the vendor, the date, the amount, and what was bought. A card
statement line is not a receipt: it shows the amount but not the purchase. Where a
receipt has genuinely been lost, the employee should say so in the claim and provide what
evidence they have; repeated lost receipts will be raised with the employee's manager.

### 3.3 Describing the expense

Every claim needs a description that would make sense to someone who was not there. "Team
dinner" is not a description; "dinner with the Austin support team after the Q3 planning
offsite, 6 attendees" is. Where an expense involves other people, the claim should say
who they were and, for client meetings, which client.

Approvers are entitled to decline a claim they cannot understand, and a claim that
returns for a better description takes longer to pay than one submitted properly.

## 4. Approval Thresholds

Approval is set by the amount of the expense.

| Amount | Approval required |
|---|---|
| $250 or less | No approval beyond submission |
| Above $250 | Manager approval |
| Above $2,000 | Finance approval, in addition to manager approval |

### 4.1 How the thresholds stack

The thresholds are cumulative, not alternative. An expense above $2,000 needs **both**
the manager's approval and Finance's; Finance approval does not replace the manager's.

### 4.2 Which amount is assessed

The threshold is assessed on the amount of the individual expense, not on the total of a
claim. A claim containing several items is assessed line by line: an expense report
totalling $600 made up of four separate items, none above $250, needs no approval beyond
submission. Conversely, one item above $250 inside an otherwise small claim pulls that
item into manager approval.

An expense must not be split into smaller pieces to stay under a threshold. Splitting a
single purchase across several claims, several cards, or several days is a deliberate
circumvention of this policy and is treated as a conduct matter under CON-01, not as an
administrative error.

### 4.3 Approval before or after

Approval is normally given when the claim is submitted, which is after the cost was
incurred. For anything substantial, and for anything the employee is unsure about,
approval should be sought **before** committing the money. An employee who incurs a large
cost and seeks approval afterwards may find it declined, and will then carry the cost
personally.

### 4.4 Who cannot approve

An employee cannot approve their own expense, and cannot approve an expense from which
they personally benefit — a manager cannot approve a dinner they attended and a colleague
claimed. Where the ordinary approver is conflicted in this way, the claim routes to the
next level up. Where an employee has no manager recorded, the claim routes to Finance.

## 5. Home Office

### 5.1 The one-time stipend

Employees receive a **one-time home office stipend of $750**, available **after 90 days
of tenure**.

The stipend covers the setup of a workspace at the employee's home location: a desk, a
chair, a lamp, a keyboard and mouse, a headset, cables, a monitor arm, and similar
equipment. It is claimed like any other expense, against receipts, and is subject to the
approval thresholds in §4 in the ordinary way.

The tenure requirement means a new employee cannot claim the stipend during their first
90 days. This is a different question from whether they need the equipment, and managers
should not attempt to route an early claim around it by expensing the items separately
under §2 — the exclusion in §5.4 applies.

### 5.2 The annual refresh

From **year 2** onwards, employees receive a **$200 annual refresh** for home office
equipment. The refresh is per calendar year, does not accumulate if unused, and is not
paid out in cash.

The refresh is for replacing and improving an existing setup rather than creating one.
Employees still in their first year use the one-time stipend in §5.1; the refresh becomes
available in the following year.

### 5.3 Office chairs

**An office chair is covered under the home office stipend and is not separately
reimbursable.**

This is stated explicitly because it is the most frequent question Finance receives. A
chair is not a special category, is not exempt from the stipend, and does not have its own
budget. An employee who has used the full stipend on other equipment has no remaining
allowance for a chair, and an employee who has not yet reached the tenure requirement in
§5.1 cannot claim one yet.

An employee who needs a specific chair for a medical or accessibility reason should raise
it with People Operations rather than claiming it as an expense. Adjustments of that kind
are handled separately from this policy and are not limited by the stipend.

### 5.4 What the stipend does not cover

The stipend does not cover a laptop or any other company-provided hardware, which is
governed by EQP-01 and is never an employee expense — see §9.1.

It does not cover rent, mortgage interest, home insurance, utilities, or internet service.
These are costs of having a home rather than costs of doing the work, and Northwind does
not reimburse them.

It does not cover general furniture or décor that happens to be in the room where an
employee works, and it does not cover equipment for a location other than the employee's
recorded home location.

## 6. Meals and Per Diem

Meals while travelling on business are reimbursed on a per diem basis:

| Travel | Per diem |
|---|---|
| Domestic | $65 per day |
| International | $90 per day |

### 6.1 How per diem works

The per diem is a fixed daily amount in place of itemised meal receipts. An employee
claims the per diem for each day of business travel and does not submit individual meal
receipts for those days.

The per diem is an upper limit for the day, not a payment. An employee whose meals cost
less claims what they spent; an employee whose meals cost more absorbs the difference.
The rate is not a stipend to be claimed in full regardless of spending.

### 6.2 Domestic and international rates

The international rate applies to travel outside the United States. For an itinerary
covering both, each day takes the rate of the location the employee is in that day.

### 6.3 Meals that are not per diem

A meal that is itself the business purpose — a client dinner, a team meal at an offsite,
a candidate lunch — is claimed as an itemised expense with a receipt and a description of
who attended, under §3.3, and is not part of the per diem. An employee should not claim
both the per diem and an itemised meal for the same meal.

Meals provided as part of a conference fee or an airline ticket are already paid for and
reduce the per diem claimable for that day.

### 6.4 Alcohol

Alcohol is not reimbursable, including within a per diem day and including as part of an
itemised client meal. See §8.1. Where a client dinner bill includes alcohol, the employee
should claim the food portion and exclude the rest.

## 7. Travel

### 7.1 Booking

Business travel should be booked through the channel Finance maintains, far enough in
advance to get a sensible price. Travel above the threshold in §4 needs the approval that
section requires before booking, not after.

### 7.2 Airfare

Airfare is reimbursed in **economy class**. **Premium economy is permitted for flights
longer than 6 hours.**

Business and first class are not reimbursable. An employee who chooses a higher class
than this policy permits may claim the amount an eligible fare would have cost and pays
the difference personally, under the incremental cost principle in §7.5.

The 6-hour test applies to the individual flight segment rather than to the total journey
including connections. An employee with a specific medical need for different seating
should raise it with People Operations rather than claiming it as an expense.

### 7.3 Accommodation

Hotels are reimbursed up to **$250 per night domestic** and **$300 per night
international**, excluding taxes and mandatory fees.

Where an event's designated conference hotel exceeds the cap, the employee should say so
in the claim; Finance will normally accept it. Where no reasonable option exists under
the cap in an expensive city on the dates required, the employee should seek approval
before booking rather than explaining afterwards.

### 7.4 Ground transport

Taxis, ride-shares, public transport, and airport parking are reimbursable at cost.
Mileage in a personal vehicle is reimbursed at the rate Finance publishes. Rental cars
are reimbursable where they are the practical option; upgrades, fuel-service charges, and
optional insurance beyond the standard cover are not.

### 7.5 Combining personal travel with business travel

Combining personal travel with a business trip is **permitted**. The employee pays the
**incremental cost**.

The incremental cost is everything the personal portion adds: extra hotel nights, the
difference between the business-only airfare and the fare actually flown, additional
ground transport, and any change fees. Northwind reimburses what the trip would have cost
had it been purely business, and the employee pays the rest.

Employees should document the comparison at the time of booking — a screenshot of the
business-only fare is enough — because reconstructing it months later is difficult and
the burden of showing the split sits with the employee.

Per diem is claimable only for the business days of a combined trip, and the caps in §7.3
apply only to the business nights.

### 7.6 Travel for a partner or family member

The cost of a partner, spouse, or family member accompanying an employee is never
reimbursable, in any part. See §8.1. Where their presence increases the cost of the
employee's own arrangements, that increase is an incremental cost the employee pays under
§7.5.

## 8. What Northwind Does Not Reimburse

### 8.1 Never reimbursable

The following are not reimbursable in any circumstances, at any amount, with any
approval:

- **Alcohol**, including within a per diem day and within a client meal.
- **Fines and penalties**, including parking tickets, speeding fines, late fees, and
  penalties for traffic or airline rule violations.
- **Personal entertainment**, including in-flight entertainment purchases, hotel movies,
  minibar items, spa and gym charges, and event tickets not part of a business purpose.
- **Spouse, partner, and family travel**, per §7.6.

These are exclusions rather than thresholds. A manager cannot approve them, Finance
cannot approve them, and a claim that includes them will have those lines removed.

### 8.2 Provided rather than expensed

Some things are not on the exclusion list but are still not expenses, because Northwind
provides them directly. A laptop is the clearest case: it is a company asset issued under
EQP-01, not something an employee buys and claims back. See §9.1.

An employee who buys a piece of company-provided equipment personally is unlikely to be
reimbursed and may end up owning hardware that cannot be used for Northwind work because
it cannot be managed by IT. Ask before buying.

### 8.3 Personal costs of working

Commuting between an employee's home and their assigned office is a personal cost and is
not reimbursable, however far the journey. Travel from the home location to a different
Northwind office at the company's request is business travel and is reimbursable.

Home internet, home utilities, and personal phone plans are not reimbursable, per §5.4.

## 9. Boundaries With Other Policies

### 9.1 Equipment (EQP-01)

**A laptop is never an employee expense.** Laptops are company-provided assets issued,
replaced, and returned under EQP-01, on the refresh cycle defined there. An employee who
needs a laptop, a replacement laptop, or an early replacement raises it through IT under
EQP-01 — not as an expense claim, and not under the home office stipend in §5.

The same applies to monitors, which EQP-01 provides on the terms set out there. The
boundary is simple: if EQP-01 issues it, it is not an expense; if the home office stipend
in §5 covers it, it is.

Peripherals that EQP-01 does not issue — keyboards, mice, headsets, cables, stands — come
out of the home office stipend under §5.1.

### 9.2 Tenure (ONB-01)

The home office stipend depends on the employee having reached the tenure requirement in
§5.1. The probation framework itself, including its length and review points, is defined
in ONB-01. An employee too new to claim the stipend should be told which of the two rules
they have run into: the stipend has a tenure requirement, and that is a different thing
from an approval being refused.

### 9.3 Remote work (REM-01)

Where an employee may work from is decided by REM-01. This policy covers what it costs.
Working temporarily from another location does not create an entitlement to a second home
office setup, and the stipend applies to the recorded home location only, per §5.4.

### 9.4 Conduct (CON-01)

Deliberately splitting expenses to avoid a threshold, claiming for costs not incurred,
claiming personal costs as business costs, or reclaiming a cost already reimbursed is
financial impropriety and is handled under CON-01 rather than as an expense dispute.

An employee who believes a claim was wrongly declined should raise it with their manager
and Finance in the first instance, and has the channels in CON-01 if that does not resolve
it.

## 10. Payment

### 10.1 Reimbursement cycle

Reimbursements are paid **twice monthly, on the 15th and on the last business day of the
month**.

A claim is paid in the first cycle after it is fully approved. A claim approved shortly
before a cycle date may not make that run; a claim awaiting an approval is not in any
cycle at all, which is the most common reason a payment an employee is expecting has not
arrived.

### 10.2 Tracking a claim

The HR portal shows the state of every claim: submitted, awaiting approval, approved, or
paid. Employees should check there before contacting Finance, because the answer is
usually that the claim is waiting on an approver.

### 10.3 Currency

Claims are reimbursed in the employee's payroll currency. Costs incurred in another
currency are converted at the rate applied by the employee's own card or bank, and the
employee should submit evidence of the rate where it is not shown on the receipt. Foreign
transaction fees on a personal card used for a business cost are reimbursable.

### 10.4 Corrections

An employee who realises a claim was wrong — a duplicate, an over-claim, a personal item
included by accident — should tell Finance. A correction raised voluntarily is an
administrative matter. The same error found later, unreported, is not.

## 11. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 11.1 Employees

Employees submit within the 45-day deadline, attach receipts above $25, describe claims
so an approver can understand them, seek approval before incurring anything substantial,
pay the incremental cost of personal travel, and never split an expense to avoid a
threshold.

### 11.2 Managers

Managers approve expenses above $250, check that the cost has a genuine business purpose
rather than only that the amount is within a limit, decline claims they cannot understand
with a reason, and route claims they cannot approve because of a conflict to the next
level up.

### 11.3 Finance

Finance owns this policy, approves expenses above $2,000, administers the reimbursement
cycle, publishes the mileage rate, maintains the booking channel, and decides late-claim
exceptions under §3.1.

### 11.4 Out of scope

This policy covers business expenses and reimbursement. It does not cover company-provided
equipment (EQP-01), where an employee may work (REM-01), tax consequences (TAX-01), or
benefits (BEN-01). Where an employee asks about a cost or benefit not described here or in
another Northwind policy, the correct answer is that the policy library does not cover it
and the question should go to Finance.

Northwind Expense and Reimbursement Policy. It is owned by Finance and is reviewed
annually. A consolidated view of every approval threshold in this policy, alongside those
from the other Northwind policy documents, is maintained in APR-01.
