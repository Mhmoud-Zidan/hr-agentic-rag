Workplace Conduct and Grievance (CON-01)

1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy sets out the standards of conduct expected at Northwind Systems Inc., how to raise a concern, what happens after one is raised, and the protection available to anyone who raises one. It is the authoritative source for the reporting channels, the acknowledgment and investigation timelines, and the anti-retaliation commitment.

Who this policy covers. Everyone who works at Northwind: employees of both entities, and contractors. Conduct obligations follow presence in the workplace rather than employment status, and a concern about a contractor's conduct — or raised by one — is handled under this policy.

Where it applies. In Northwind offices, at any location where an employee is working under REM-01, in every Northwind system and channel, and at work-related events wherever they are held. A remote workplace is a workplace.

1.1 Definitions

Concern. Any report that something is wrong: a conduct issue, a grievance about treatment, a safety problem, or a suspicion of impropriety. This policy uses one word for all of them because a person raising something should not have to classify it correctly to be heard.

HR Business Partner. The People Operations contact assigned to a department, and one of the three reporting channels in §2.

Ethics line. The anonymous reporting channel described in §2.3.

Retaliation. Any detriment imposed on someone because they raised a concern or took part in an investigation. See §5.

Business day. A day on which Northwind's US offices are scheduled to be open, excluding weekends and company holidays. The timelines in §4 are counted in business days.

2. How to Raise a Concern

There are three reporting channels, and an employee may use whichever they prefer:

ChannelUse it when
Direct managerThe concern is about work, a colleague, or a decision, and you are comfortable raising it with your manager.
HR Business PartnerThe concern involves your manager, is sensitive, or you would rather it went outside your reporting line.
Anonymous ethics lineYou want to report without identifying yourself, or the other channels feel unsafe.

2.1 There is no required order

The channels are alternatives, not a sequence. Nobody is required to raise a concern with their manager first, and choosing to go straight to an HR Business Partner or the ethics line is never held against the person reporting. An employee who is told they should have gone to their manager first has been told something this policy does not say.

2.2 Raising a concern with a manager

A manager who receives a concern must take it seriously, must not attempt to resolve a matter that falls under §3 informally, and must not promise confidentiality they cannot deliver. Managers who are unsure what to do with something raised with them should contact their HR Business Partner the same day rather than sitting on it.

2.3 The anonymous ethics line

The ethics line accepts reports without identifying the reporter. It is operated so that People Operations receives the substance of a report without the identity of the person who made it.

Anonymity has a practical cost worth understanding: an investigation cannot come back to ask a follow-up question, which sometimes limits how far a report can be taken. Where someone is willing to be identified to People Operations but not to the person the concern is about, saying so usually produces a better outcome than full anonymity. That choice belongs to the person reporting.

2.4 What to include

A useful report says what happened, when, where, who was involved, and whether anyone else saw it. A report that lacks these is still worth making — People Operations would far rather receive an incomplete report than none.

3. Matters That Must Be Escalated

Four categories must be escalated to People Operations and cannot be handled informally by a manager:

  • Harassment
  • Discrimination
  • Safety
  • Financial impropriety

3.1 Why these are mandatory

These four carry legal obligations, affect people beyond the individuals involved, or both. A manager cannot assess whether a pattern exists across teams, cannot weigh a report against others already received, and cannot provide the procedural protection an investigation requires. Handling one of these quietly at team level is not discretion; it is a failure to apply this policy.

3.2 The obligation is on the manager

A manager who becomes aware of a matter in any of the four categories must escalate it, even if the person raising it asks them not to. This is the one place where a manager cannot follow the wishes of the person reporting, and they should say so plainly and early — before the person shares more than they want to.

The right words are: "I need to tell you that if this is what I think it is, I have to pass it to People Operations. I can tell you what happens next, and you can decide how much you want to tell me."

3.3 Escalation is not accusation

Escalating a matter is not a finding that anyone did anything wrong. It routes the concern to people equipped to look at it properly. Managers who hesitate to escalate because it feels like an accusation should treat escalation as neutral, because it is.

3.4 Financial impropriety

Financial impropriety includes deliberate manipulation of expense claims — splitting to avoid a threshold, claiming costs not incurred, claiming personal costs as business costs — as EXP-01 describes, and any misuse of Northwind funds or corporate cards. It also covers concealing a matter that should have been reported, such as the deliberate non-reporting of a security incident under SEC-01.

An honest mistake reported promptly is not impropriety. The distinction is concealment and intent, not the amount involved.

4. What Happens After a Concern Is Raised

Two timelines apply once People Operations receives a concern:

StageTimeline
Acknowledgment by HRWithin 2 business days
InvestigationTarget of 15 business days

4.1 Acknowledgment

People Operations acknowledges a concern within 2 business days of receiving it. The acknowledgment confirms the concern has been received, names the person handling it, and explains what happens next.

Acknowledgment is not an assessment and says nothing about the outcome. Its purpose is that nobody who raises something is left wondering whether it reached anyone — the silence after raising a difficult thing is itself corrosive.

Where a report is made anonymously through the ethics line and includes no route for reply, People Operations cannot acknowledge to an individual and instead records receipt internally.

4.2 Investigation

Investigations target completion within 15 business days. The target covers gathering the facts, speaking to those involved, and reaching a conclusion.

It is a target rather than a guarantee, and complex matters, matters involving several people, or matters where a key participant is unavailable can take longer. Where an investigation will exceed the target, People Operations tells the person who raised the concern before the target passes and gives a revised expectation. An investigation running late is not a reason to stop communicating; it is a reason to communicate more.

4.3 How investigations are conducted

An investigation is conducted by someone independent of the matter. Both the person raising the concern and anyone it is about will be heard. Notes are kept, and the conclusion and its basis are recorded.

People are told what they need to know to take part and no more. Someone interviewed as a witness is not told the full substance of the concern, and the outcome is shared with the people it directly affects rather than with a team.

4.4 Outcomes

An investigation may conclude that the concern is upheld, not upheld, or that the evidence does not allow a conclusion either way. The third outcome is a real one and is not a polite way of saying the concern was disbelieved.

Where a concern is upheld, the action taken depends on the seriousness and may include coaching, a change in working arrangements, a formal warning, or termination of employment. The person who raised the concern is told that action has been taken, though they may not be told exactly what, because the person it concerns has their own right to confidentiality.

4.5 If you are unhappy with the outcome

Someone unhappy with a conclusion may ask People Operations to review it, setting out why. A review looks at whether the process was sound and whether the conclusion followed from the evidence; it is not a rerun of the investigation.

5. Anti-Retaliation

The protection against retaliation at Northwind is absolute.

5.1 What absolute means

It means there are no conditions on it. Retaliation is prohibited regardless of whether the concern was upheld, whether it was well-founded, whether the person reporting was mistaken, how senior anyone involved is, and how disruptive the report was.

A person who raises a concern in good faith and turns out to be wrong is protected in exactly the same way as one who turns out to be right. Any other rule would require people to be certain before speaking, and certainty is rarely available to the person who notices something first.

5.2 What retaliation looks like

Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, or a change in duties; exclusion from meetings, projects, or opportunities; a sudden change in how performance is assessed; withdrawal of a flexible or remote working arrangement; and social exclusion or hostility from colleagues.

Much retaliation is quiet. Being left off an invitation list, given less interesting work, or treated with new coolness is retaliation as much as anything formal, and is harder to evidence, which is why a change in treatment after a concern is raised is examined on its own terms rather than requiring proof of motive.

5.3 Reporting retaliation

Retaliation is itself a concern under this policy and is raised through any of the channels in §2. It is treated with the same timelines in §4 and, because it goes to whether the process can be trusted at all, is given priority.

5.4 Protection for participants

The protection covers anyone who takes part in an investigation, not only the person who raised the concern. A witness who gives an account is protected on the same terms.

5.5 Bad-faith reports

A report made in good faith that turns out to be unfounded is protected without qualification. A report fabricated deliberately to harm someone is a conduct matter in its own right. The bar for treating a report as bad faith is deliberate fabrication, not inaccuracy, and People Operations applies that bar carefully — a policy that punishes honest mistakes stops receiving honest reports.

6. Standards of Conduct

6.1 The expectation

Everyone at Northwind is expected to treat colleagues, customers, candidates, and partners with respect. That means communicating directly and without hostility, disagreeing with ideas rather than attacking people, and recognising that the effect of behaviour matters more than the intent behind it.

6.2 Harassment and discrimination

Harassment and discrimination are prohibited without exception. Discrimination means treating someone less favourably because of who they are. Harassment means unwanted conduct that violates someone's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, or humiliating environment.

Neither requires intent. A pattern of jokes at someone's expense is harassment whatever the intention, and a decision that disadvantages a group is discrimination whether or not anyone meant it to. Both must be escalated under §3.

6.3 Conduct in remote and written channels

Chat, email, video calls, and shared documents are workplaces. Conduct standards apply identically in them, and the written record makes behaviour more visible rather than less. A comment in a channel is not less serious than the same comment in a room.

6.4 Managers

Managers are held to the standards in this section and to an additional one: they influence the conditions in which everyone else works. A manager who tolerates poor behaviour in their team has permitted it, and that is assessed as part of their own conduct.

6.5 Safety

Anyone who becomes aware of a safety risk — in an office, at an event, or arising from someone's behaviour — must raise it. Safety is one of the mandatory escalation categories in §3, and raising a risk that turns out to be nothing is always the right call.

7. Confidentiality

7.1 What is kept confidential

Concerns are handled on a need-to-know basis. The identity of the person raising a concern, the substance of the report, and the outcome are shared only with those who need them to investigate or to act.

7.2 What cannot be promised

Northwind cannot promise absolute confidentiality for a matter it must investigate. The person a concern is about generally has to know enough to respond to it, and a matter in one of the categories in §3 must be escalated regardless of what the reporter prefers.

People Operations explains the limits of confidentiality at the start rather than after a disclosure. Anyone who wants to understand those limits before deciding how much to say can ask first and decide afterwards; that is a reasonable thing to do and People Operations expects it.

7.3 Discussing an ongoing matter

Those involved in an investigation are asked not to discuss it while it is in progress, to protect its integrity and everyone's privacy. This is not a gag: nobody is prevented from seeking support from a trusted person, from their HR Business Partner, or from their own adviser.

8. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

8.1 Everyone

Everyone at Northwind meets the standards in §6, raises concerns through any channel in §2, takes part honestly in investigations, and never retaliates against anyone who raises a concern.

8.2 Managers

Managers receive concerns without defensiveness, escalate the four categories in §3 even when asked not to, explain the limits of confidentiality before a disclosure rather than after, contact their HR Business Partner the same day when unsure, and watch for quiet retaliation in their team after a concern has been raised.

8.3 People Operations

People Operations owns this policy, acknowledges concerns within 2 business days, conducts or assigns investigations against the 15 business day target, communicates when a target will be missed, operates the ethics line, and enforces the anti-retaliation commitment in §5.

8.4 Out of scope

This policy covers conduct, grievances, and how concerns are handled. It does not cover performance management, which is handled between an employee and their manager with People Operations' support, and it does not cover the substance of the other policies — a disagreement about a leave decision is raised here, but the leave rules themselves are in PTO-01. Where a question is not answered here or in another Northwind policy, the correct answer is that the policy library does not cover it and the question should go to People Operations.
