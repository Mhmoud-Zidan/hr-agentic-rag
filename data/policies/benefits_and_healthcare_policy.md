# Benefits and Healthcare Policy (BEN-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy describes the benefits Northwind Systems Inc. provides, who is eligible for
each, when coverage begins, and when elections can be changed. It is the authoritative
source for every benefits eligibility threshold in the Northwind policy library.

**Who this policy covers.** Employees of Northwind Systems Inc. in the United States.
Employees of Northwind Systems Ltd. (Dublin, Ireland) are covered by a separate schedule,
set out in §9.

**Contractors.** Contractors are **not eligible for any benefit** described in this
policy. See §10.3.

### 1.1 The two thresholds

Benefits eligibility at Northwind turns on scheduled hours, and there are **two different
thresholds** that are easy to confuse:

- **30 hours per week or more** — eligible for medical, dental, and vision coverage.
- **20 hours per week or more** — eligible for the wellness stipend.

Retirement has no hours threshold at all. An employee scheduled below 30 hours is
therefore not simply "ineligible for benefits": they may be eligible for some and not
others, and the correct answer depends on which benefit is being asked about. See §10.1.

### 1.2 Definitions

**Scheduled hours.** The weekly hours recorded against an employee in the HR system, not
the hours actually worked in a given week. Eligibility follows the schedule.

**Waiting period.** The time between an employee's start date and the date coverage
begins. See §3.

**Election.** An employee's choice of plan and coverage tier. Elections are made at
onboarding, at open enrollment, or after a qualifying life event, and not otherwise. See
§5 and §6.

**Coverage tier.** Who a plan covers: the employee alone, the employee and a spouse or
partner, or the employee and family.

**Plan year.** January 1 to December 31, matching Northwind's fiscal year.

### 1.3 Related documents

- ONB-01 — onboarding and probation. Benefits are active during probation, subject to the
  waiting period in §3.
- PTO-01 — paid time off, sick leave, parental leave, and unpaid leave, including how
  coverage is treated during a leave.
- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work.
- TAX-01 — work location and tax compliance, relevant on an entity transfer.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance, including how to raise a concern.

## 2. Eligibility

### 2.1 Health coverage

Medical, dental, and vision coverage is available to employees scheduled **30 or more
hours per week**.

The threshold is applied to scheduled hours as recorded in the HR system. An employee
scheduled below it is not eligible for medical, dental, or vision coverage, regardless of
tenure, performance, role, or how many hours they happen to work in a particular week.

### 2.2 Retirement

The retirement plan has **no hours threshold**. Every employee is eligible from day one,
including part-time employees scheduled below the health threshold. See §7.

### 2.3 Wellness stipend

The wellness stipend is available to all employees scheduled **20 or more hours per
week**. See §8.3.

### 2.4 A change in scheduled hours

Eligibility follows scheduled hours, so a change in an employee's schedule can change
what they are eligible for.

An employee whose hours increase to or above the health threshold becomes eligible, and
the change of hours is a qualifying life event under §6 that allows them to elect
coverage outside open enrollment. An employee whose hours decrease below the threshold
loses eligibility, and People Operations will contact them about continuation options
before coverage ends.

Employees considering a change to their working pattern — including on return from
parental leave under PTO-01 — should ask People Operations what it would mean for their
benefits **before** agreeing the change, not after.

### 2.5 Eligibility is not enrollment

Being eligible does not mean being covered. An eligible employee must make an election
within the window in §5.2 to be enrolled. An employee who misses the window is eligible
but not covered, and must wait for the next open enrollment or a qualifying life event.

## 3. Waiting Period and When Coverage Starts

Health coverage begins on the **first of the month following 30 days of employment**.

### 3.1 How the waiting period works

The employee serves 30 days from their start date, and coverage then begins on the first
day of the next month. The date is determined by this rule and cannot be brought forward
by a manager, by People Operations, or by the employee electing early.

The practical effect is that the gap between starting and being covered is never less
than 30 days and can be longer, depending on where in the month the employee started.
New joiners should plan for this and should not schedule non-urgent medical appointments
in that window assuming coverage.

### 3.2 Benefits during probation

**Benefits are active during probation**, subject to this waiting period. Probation is
defined in ONB-01 and has no effect on benefits eligibility: an employee inside their
probation period whose waiting period has completed is covered in the normal way.

This is deliberately different from the treatment of paid time off, which accrues during
probation but cannot be taken until the point defined in PTO-01. Benefits and leave
follow different rules during an employee's early period, and an answer about one is not
an answer about the other.

### 3.3 Retirement has no waiting period

The retirement plan is available from **day 1**, with no waiting period at all. See §7.1.

### 3.4 Coverage end

Coverage ends on the last day of the month in which employment ends. People Operations
provides information about continuation options at that point. Retirement contributions
stop with the final payroll; the account itself belongs to the employee, because vesting
is immediate under §7.3.

## 4. Medical, Dental, and Vision

### 4.1 What is offered

Northwind offers medical, dental, and vision plans through its benefits provider.
Employees elect each independently: an employee may take medical and decline dental, or
any other combination, and each election has its own coverage tier.

Plan documents, provider networks, contribution amounts, and the summary of benefits are
published in the HR portal and are updated for each plan year ahead of open enrollment.
Those documents govern what a plan actually pays for; this policy governs eligibility,
timing, and elections.

### 4.2 Coverage tiers

Each plan may be elected for the employee alone, the employee plus a spouse or domestic
partner, or the employee plus family. The contribution differs by tier and is published
with the plan documents.

### 4.3 Dependants

Eligible dependants are a spouse or domestic partner and children, on the terms in the
plan documents. Adding a dependant outside open enrollment requires a qualifying life
event under §6.

Northwind may ask for evidence of a dependant relationship when one is added. This is a
requirement of the benefits provider rather than a Northwind judgement about an
employee's family.

### 4.4 Questions about claims and coverage

Questions about whether a specific treatment is covered, about a claim, or about a
provider network go to the benefits provider, whose contact details are in the HR portal.
People Operations administers eligibility and elections and cannot answer clinical or
claims questions, and will not ask an employee to disclose a medical reason for a query.

## 5. Open Enrollment

Open enrollment runs **November 1 to November 21** each year, and elections made during
it take effect on **January 1**.

### 5.1 What open enrollment is for

Open enrollment is the annual window in which any eligible employee may start, stop, or
change any election for the following plan year — switch plans, change coverage tier, add
or remove a dependant, or change contribution amounts.

Outside this window, elections can only be changed after a qualifying life event under
§6. This is a constraint of the benefits provider and of the tax treatment of the plans,
not a Northwind administrative preference, and it cannot be waived.

### 5.2 New joiners

A new employee elects coverage when they join, within the window People Operations
communicates during onboarding, with coverage starting per §3. A new joiner does not wait
for open enrollment to elect for the first time.

An employee who misses their joining window is not covered and must wait for the next
open enrollment or a qualifying life event. Managers should check during onboarding that
a new joiner has completed their elections; ONB-01 includes this in the onboarding
sequence.

### 5.3 If you do nothing

An employee who makes no changes during open enrollment normally carries their existing
elections into the new plan year. Where a plan is discontinued or materially changed,
People Operations will say so before the window opens and will explain what happens to
employees enrolled in it. Employees should not assume that doing nothing is always
neutral — read the communication each year.

### 5.4 Dates are fixed

The open enrollment window does not move and is not extended for individual employees.
Employees who will be on leave or travelling during the window should complete their
elections before they go. People Operations publicises the window well in advance for
this reason.

## 6. Qualifying Life Events

An employee has **30 days from a qualifying life event** to change their elections.

### 6.1 What counts

Qualifying life events include marriage, divorce, the birth or adoption of a child, the
death of a dependant, a change in the employee's scheduled hours that changes eligibility
under §2.4, and a spouse or partner gaining or losing coverage elsewhere.

The list above is the common set. Where an employee is unsure whether a change qualifies,
they should contact People Operations promptly rather than waiting to find out — the
30-day clock runs from the event, not from the date the employee establishes whether it
counts.

### 6.2 The 30-day window

The window runs 30 days from the date of the event. An employee who misses it must wait
for the next open enrollment, however sympathetic the circumstances and however obviously
the event qualified.

This is the single most consequential deadline in this policy. A new parent who does not
add their child within 30 days of the birth has no route to add them until the following
January. People Operations reminds employees of this where it knows about the event, but
the obligation to act sits with the employee.

### 6.3 What can be changed

A qualifying life event allows changes consistent with the event: adding a new child,
adding a spouse on marriage, removing a dependant who has gained coverage elsewhere, or
electing coverage on becoming eligible. It does not reopen every election — an employee
cannot use an unrelated event as an opportunity to switch plans for reasons of their own.

### 6.4 Evidence

Northwind may need evidence of the event, such as a birth certificate or a marriage
certificate, on the benefits provider's terms. Employees should not delay notifying
People Operations while waiting for a document; notify first, provide evidence after.

## 7. Retirement

### 7.1 Eligibility

The retirement plan is available from **day 1 of employment**, with no waiting period and
no hours threshold. Every employee is eligible, including part-time employees scheduled
below the health threshold in §2.1.

### 7.2 Employer match

Northwind matches **100% of the first 3% of pay contributed, and 50% of the next 2%**.

An employee contributing 5% of pay therefore receives the full available match: the first
3% matched in full, and the next 2% matched at half. An employee contributing less than
5% receives a proportionally smaller match, and an employee contributing more than 5%
receives no additional match on the excess — though they may still wish to contribute
more for their own reasons.

Employees who can afford to contribute at least enough to receive the full match are
generally advised in the plan materials to do so, because the match is additional
compensation that is otherwise forgone. That is general information about how the plan
works and not personal financial advice; Northwind does not provide personal financial
advice and employees should consult their own adviser.

### 7.3 Vesting

Employer match contributions vest **immediately**. There is no vesting schedule, no cliff,
and no service requirement. Every matched contribution belongs to the employee from the
moment it is made, and an employee who leaves Northwind takes the full value with them
regardless of tenure.

### 7.4 Changing contributions

Employees may change their contribution rate through the plan provider at any time, and
the change takes effect from the next practicable payroll. Contribution changes are not
tied to open enrollment and do not require a qualifying life event.

### 7.5 Contribution limits

Annual contribution limits are set by law and published by the plan provider each year.
The provider administers those limits; Northwind does not set them and cannot raise them.

## 8. FSA and Wellness

### 8.1 Dependent care FSA

Northwind offers a dependent care flexible spending account with an annual limit of
**$5,000**.

The FSA allows an employee to set aside pre-tax pay for eligible dependent care costs.
Elections are made at open enrollment or after a qualifying life event, on the same
timing as every other election.

### 8.2 FSA rules come from the plan

The FSA is governed by the plan documents and by the tax rules that make it work,
including the rules on which costs are eligible and what happens to unused funds at the
end of the plan year. Employees should read the plan documents before electing an amount,
and should elect conservatively where they are unsure, because the rules on unused funds
are stricter than most people expect.

### 8.3 Wellness stipend

Northwind provides a wellness stipend of **$50 per month** to all employees scheduled
**20 or more hours per week**.

The stipend may be used for gym membership, fitness classes, mental health apps and
services, and similar wellbeing costs. It is claimed through the HR portal.

The wellness threshold is lower than the health coverage threshold in §2.1, and this is
deliberate. A part-time employee scheduled between the two thresholds is eligible for the
wellness stipend and not for medical, dental, or vision coverage — one record, two
different answers, both correct. See §10.1.

### 8.4 The stipend does not accumulate

The wellness stipend is a monthly entitlement. Unused months do not accumulate, are not
paid out in cash, and cannot be claimed retrospectively beyond the period the HR portal
allows.

## 9. Northwind Systems Ltd. (Ireland) Benefits Schedule

Employees of **Northwind Systems Ltd. in Dublin are covered by a separate benefits
schedule** and not by the US provisions in §2 through §8.

### 9.1 Why the schedule is separate

Northwind Systems Ltd. is a separate legal entity in a different jurisdiction, with its
own statutory requirements, its own healthcare system, and its own retirement framework.
The US plans described in this policy are not available to Irish employees, and the Irish
arrangements have no US equivalent.

This is not a difference in generosity. It is a difference in what the two jurisdictions
require and in what can be provided in each.

### 9.2 What applies to Irish employees

Irish employees are covered by the Irish benefits schedule maintained by the EMEA People
Operations team and published in the HR portal. That schedule sets out the health
arrangements, the retirement arrangements, and the statutory entitlements applicable to
employees of the Irish entity, along with their enrollment windows.

Where this policy and the Irish schedule differ for an employee of the Irish entity, the
Irish schedule governs.

### 9.3 What is common across both entities

The wellness stipend in §8.3 is offered to employees of both entities on the same hours
threshold. Practical administration — where to find plan documents, how to raise a
question, and the confidentiality of benefits queries — is the same in both.

### 9.4 Transfers between entities

An employee who transfers between the US and Irish entities moves onto the receiving
entity's schedule. Their elections do not transfer, because the plans are different
plans, and they make new elections on the receiving entity's terms and timing.

A transfer is a change of employment rather than a travel arrangement; the circumstances
in which one is used are described in REM-01, and the tax consequences in TAX-01.
Employees considering a transfer should ask EMEA People Operations for the Irish schedule
before deciding, since the benefits difference is a material part of the decision.

### 9.5 Working across entities temporarily

An employee of one entity who works temporarily from the other's country remains on their
own entity's benefits schedule. A short period of work in another country does not change
which entity employs someone, and it does not change their benefits. The rules governing
whether such an arrangement is permitted at all are in REM-01.

## 10. Part-Time Employees, Contractors, and Ineligibility

### 10.1 Part-time employees below the health threshold

A part-time employee scheduled **below 30 hours per week** is eligible for the
**retirement plan only** and is **not eligible for medical, dental, or vision coverage**.

If that employee is scheduled 20 or more hours per week, they are also eligible for the
wellness stipend under §8.3.

So the complete answer for a part-time employee between the two thresholds is: retirement
yes, wellness yes, health coverage no. Anyone answering only "not eligible for benefits"
has answered the question wrongly, and should say which benefit they mean.

### 10.2 Part-time employees at or above the health threshold

A part-time employee scheduled at or above 30 hours per week is eligible for health
coverage on the same terms as a full-time employee. The threshold is the only test; there
is no separate full-time requirement.

### 10.3 Contractors

Contractors are **not eligible for any benefit** in this policy — not health coverage, not
the retirement plan, not the FSA, and not the wellness stipend. This is a consequence of
not being an employee, and it does not vary with how long the engagement has run or how
many hours a contractor works.

A contractor asking about benefits should be routed to the engaging manager and People
Operations rather than given an eligibility answer based on their hours. Their hours are
not the reason, and answering as though they were invites the follow-up question of what
would happen if they worked more.

### 10.4 Common misreadings

Three errors recur often enough to name:

- Treating "part-time" as a single eligibility category. It is not; the thresholds in
  §1.1 do the work, and part-time employees can sit on either side of either one.
- Treating the wellness threshold and the health threshold as the same number.
- Answering a contractor's eligibility question by reference to scheduled hours rather
  than to employment status.

## 11. Interaction With Leave and Other Policies

### 11.1 Paid leave (PTO-01)

An employee on paid leave — PTO, sick leave, bereavement leave, or parental leave —
remains covered, and elections and contributions continue unchanged.

### 11.2 Unpaid leave (PTO-01)

An employee on an unpaid leave of absence remains employed and remains eligible, but
because there is no pay, contributions that are ordinarily deducted from pay cannot be.
People Operations contacts the employee before an unpaid leave begins to arrange how
contributions will be handled during it. Retirement contributions pause with pay and
resume on return.

Employees should settle this before the leave starts rather than during it, because a
missed contribution can affect coverage.

### 11.3 Probation (ONB-01)

Benefits are active during probation, subject only to the waiting period in §3. See §3.2.

### 11.4 A change of working pattern

A change in scheduled hours can change eligibility in both directions, per §2.4, and is a
qualifying life event under §6. An employee returning from parental leave who wants to
change their hours should raise it with People Operations before agreeing the change, as
PTO-01 also advises.

### 11.5 Confidentiality

Benefits elections and any medical information connected to them are confidential.
Managers do not see an employee's elections and do not need to. An employee is never
required to tell their manager why they need a benefit, and People Operations will not
disclose it.

An employee who believes benefits information has been handled improperly, or that a
benefits decision was unfair, has the channels in CON-01.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

**I work part-time. Do I get benefits?**
It depends which benefit. Retirement yes, from day 1, with no hours threshold. Wellness
stipend yes, if scheduled 20 or more hours per week. Medical, dental, and vision only if
scheduled 30 or more hours per week. See §10.1.

**When does my health coverage start?**
The first of the month following 30 days of employment. See §3.

**Am I covered during probation?**
Yes, subject to the waiting period. Probation does not affect benefits. See §3.2.

**When can I change my elections?**
During open enrollment, November 1 to 21, effective January 1 — or within 30 days of a
qualifying life event. See §5 and §6.

**I just had a child. How long do I have to add them?**
30 days from the birth. Missing it means waiting until the next open enrollment. See §6.2.

**What is the retirement match?**
100% of the first 3% of pay you contribute, and 50% of the next 2%. See §7.2.

**When does the match vest?**
Immediately. There is no vesting schedule. See §7.3.

**I'm a contractor. What am I eligible for?**
Nothing in this policy. See §10.3.

**I work in Dublin. Does this policy apply to me?**
The wellness stipend does. Everything else is in the separate Irish schedule. See §9.

**My hours are changing. What happens to my coverage?**
Eligibility follows scheduled hours and the change is a qualifying life event. Ask People
Operations before the change takes effect. See §2.4.

**Does my manager see my elections?**
No. See §11.5.

## 13. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 13.1 Employees

Employees complete their elections within the joining window or open enrollment, notify
People Operations within 30 days of a qualifying life event, read the plan documents
before electing an FSA amount, and ask People Operations about benefits consequences
before agreeing a change to their working pattern.

### 13.2 Managers

Managers check during onboarding that a new joiner has completed their elections, route
benefits questions to People Operations rather than answering from memory, and do not
ask employees about the content of their elections or the reasons for them.

### 13.3 People Operations

People Operations owns this policy, administers eligibility and elections, runs open
enrollment, maintains plan documents in the HR portal, contacts employees whose
eligibility is changing, and arranges contribution handling ahead of an unpaid leave.
EMEA People Operations maintains the Irish schedule in §9.

### 13.4 Out of scope

This policy covers the benefits Northwind provides and who is eligible for them. It does
not cover clinical or claims questions, which go to the benefits provider, and it does not
cover personal financial advice. Where an employee asks about a benefit not described here
or in another Northwind policy, the correct answer is that the policy library does not
cover it and the question should go to People Operations.

### 13.5 Version and review

This is version v4.2, effective 2026-01-01. It supersedes all prior versions of the
Northwind Benefits and Healthcare Policy. It is owned by People Operations and is reviewed
annually ahead of open enrollment. A consolidated view of every threshold in this policy,
alongside those from the other Northwind policy documents, is maintained in APR-01.
