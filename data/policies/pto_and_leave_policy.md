# PTO and Leave Policy (PTO-01)

## 1. Purpose, Scope, and Definitions

This policy governs all paid and unpaid time away from work at Northwind Systems Inc.
It defines how paid time off (PTO) is earned, requested, approved, carried over, and
paid out, and it sets out the separate rules for sick leave, bereavement leave,
parental leave, and unpaid leave of absence. It is the authoritative source for every
leave balance, notice period, and approval threshold referenced elsewhere in the
Northwind policy library.

**Who this policy covers.** This policy applies to all employees of Northwind Systems
Inc. in the United States, including full-time and part-time employees, at every
office location and for employees working remotely. Employees of Northwind Systems
Ltd. (Dublin, Ireland) are covered by the Irish statutory leave schedule maintained by
the EMEA People Operations team; where this policy and the Irish schedule differ, the
Irish schedule governs for those employees.

**Who this policy does not cover.** Contractors are not employees of Northwind Systems
Inc. and do not accrue PTO, sick leave, bereavement leave, or parental leave under this
policy. Contractor time away from work is governed entirely by the contractor's
statement of work and the terms agreed with the engaging vendor. A contractor record
in the HR system carries a zero PTO balance and a zero sick balance by design; those
zeros are not an error and are not a balance that can be increased. Requests from
contractors for leave entitlements should be escalated to the engaging manager and
People Operations rather than processed as leave requests.

### 1.1 Definitions

**Paid time off (PTO).** A single pooled bank of paid days used for vacation, personal
business, religious observance, appointments, and any other planned absence that is not
covered by one of the separate leave types defined in this policy. Northwind does not
maintain separate vacation and personal-day banks; there is one PTO bank per employee.

**Sick leave.** A separate bank of paid days for the employee's own illness, injury, or
medical appointments, and for the care of a family member in the same circumstances.
Sick leave is described in §6 and is not part of the PTO bank.

**Business day.** A day on which Northwind's US offices are scheduled to be open,
excluding weekends and company holidays. All notice periods in this policy are stated
in business days unless a section says otherwise. Notice periods stated in business
days are counted from the day the request is submitted in the HR portal, not from the
day it is approved.

**Calendar day.** Every day including weekends and holidays. Calendar days are used for
parental leave, unpaid leave, and probation-related counts.

**Consecutive days.** A single unbroken block of requested PTO. Weekends and company
holidays that fall inside a requested block do not consume PTO days, but they do not
break the block for the purpose of the consecutive-day approval rules in §3.

**Direct manager.** The manager recorded in the employee's HR record. Where an employee
has no recorded manager, the approval path terminates and the request is routed to
People Operations, as described in §3.4.

**Department head.** The senior leader accountable for the department in which the
employee sits. Department head approval is a second, additional approval, not a
substitute for direct manager approval.

**HR / People Operations.** People Operations is Northwind's human resources function.
Where this policy or any other Northwind policy refers to HR, it means People
Operations; the two terms are interchangeable and refer to the same team.

### 1.2 How to use this document

Each section below stands on its own. Accrual rules are in §2. Notice periods and
approvals are in §3. Carryover and expiration are in §4. Payout on termination is in
§5. Sick, bereavement, parental, and unpaid leave are in §6, §7, §8, and §9
respectively. Interactions with probation, remote work, and benefits eligibility are
in §10. Worked examples are in §11 and frequently asked questions are in §12.

### 1.3 Related documents

- ONB-01 — onboarding and probation, including the probation period that restricts PTO
  usage under §2.4 of this policy.
- REM-01 — remote and hybrid work, including the rules for working from a location
  other than the employee's home office. Working remotely is not leave and is not
  requested through this policy.
- BEN-01 — benefits and healthcare, including eligibility thresholds that are separate
  from leave accrual.
- TAX-01 — work location and tax compliance, relevant when time away is combined with
  work from another state or country.
- APR-01 — the consolidated approvals and escalation matrix, which restates every
  approval threshold in this policy alongside the thresholds from other documents.
- CON-01 — workplace conduct and grievance, including the route for raising a concern
  about how a leave request was handled.

## 2. PTO Accrual

### 2.1 Accrual rate

Full-time employees accrue PTO at a rate of **1.67 days per month**, which is the
monthly equivalent of **20 days per year**. Accrual is credited monthly and is visible
in the employee's balance in the HR portal.

Part-time employees accrue PTO **pro-rated by their scheduled hours**. A part-time
employee's monthly accrual is the full-time rate of 1.67 days per month scaled by the
ratio of the employee's scheduled weekly hours to full-time scheduled weekly hours as
recorded in the HR system. Pro-rated accrual is the only difference in how PTO is
earned by part-time employees; every other rule in this policy — notice periods,
approvals, carryover, expiration, and payout — applies to part-time employees on the
same terms as full-time employees.

Accrual is a function of scheduled hours, not hours actually worked in a given month.
An employee who works additional hours in a month does not accrue additional PTO, and
an employee who takes PTO during a month continues to accrue for that month.

### 2.2 When accrual starts

PTO accrual starts on the **first day of employment**. There is no waiting period
before PTO begins to accrue, and there is no separate accrual start date for part-time
employees or for employees who join mid-month.

This is deliberately different from the waiting period that applies to health benefits
under BEN-01, and from the probation period defined in ONB-01. A new employee is
accruing PTO from day one even though, as §2.4 explains, that employee cannot yet take
it.

### 2.3 Balances

An employee's PTO balance is the total PTO accrued, plus any carryover credited under
§4, minus PTO already taken and minus PTO already approved for future dates. Requests
that are submitted but not yet approved are shown as pending and are not deducted from
the balance until approval.

PTO cannot be taken in advance of accrual. An employee may not carry a negative PTO
balance, and a request that would take the balance below zero will be declined on
balance grounds regardless of how much notice was given. Where a request exceeds the
available balance, the employee may reduce the number of days requested, move the
request to a later date by which enough PTO will have accrued, or apply for unpaid
leave under §9 if the absence cannot be moved.

Balance is assessed against the dates requested, not against the date of submission.
An employee whose balance is currently insufficient but who will have accrued enough
PTO by the requested dates may still have the request approved, provided every other
rule in this policy is satisfied.

### 2.4 Accrual during probation

PTO **accrues during probation but cannot be taken before day 91** of employment. The
length of the probation period itself is defined in ONB-01, not here.

An employee inside the probation window will therefore see a growing PTO balance in the
HR portal and will still have PTO requests declined if the requested dates fall on or
before day 90. This is not a defect in the balance; the balance is correct and the days
are genuinely earned. They simply are not yet available to take.

An employee in probation may submit a request for dates that fall on or after day 91,
provided the notice requirements in §3 are met and the balance will be sufficient by
those dates. Managers should not approve PTO for dates on or before day 90, and the HR
portal will flag such requests. Where an extension of probation has been granted under
ONB-01, the PTO usability date remains day 91 of employment and does not move with the
extension.

### 2.5 Accrual during other leave

An employee on paid sick leave, bereavement leave, or parental leave continues to
accrue PTO at their normal rate. An employee on unpaid leave under §9 does not accrue
PTO for the duration of the unpaid leave; accrual resumes on the employee's first day
back at work.

## 3. Requesting PTO: Notice Periods and Approvals

### 3.1 Notice periods

Every PTO request must be submitted in the HR portal with at least the minimum notice
for the size of the request. Notice is measured in business days between the date of
submission and the first requested day of absence.

| Length of request | Minimum notice |
|---|---|
| 1–2 days | 3 business days |
| 3–5 days | 10 business days |
| 6 or more days | 20 business days |

The tier is set by the number of PTO days requested in the block, not by the number of
calendar days it spans. A request that spans a weekend but consumes three PTO days
falls in the 3–5 day tier and requires 10 business days of notice.

Notice periods exist so that teams can plan coverage. They are a requirement of the
policy, not a guideline, and they are assessed independently of the employee's balance.
A request can have a sufficient balance and still fail on notice. This is the most
common reason a well-intentioned request is declined: an employee with plenty of PTO
asks on Monday for three days off the following week and falls short of the 10 business
days that a 3–5 day request requires.

An employee whose request fails the notice test has three practical options: move the
requested dates later so that the notice period is satisfied, reduce the request to a
length whose notice tier is already satisfied, or ask their manager to seek an
exception under §3.5. Managers should state which of these applies when declining a
request so that the employee can act on it.

### 3.2 Approvers

The **direct manager** is the approver for every PTO request. A request is not approved
until the direct manager has approved it in the HR portal; verbal or chat agreement is
not an approval and does not deduct the days from the balance.

Requests of **10 or more consecutive days** require a second approval from the
**department head**, in addition to the direct manager's approval. The department head
approval is sequential: the direct manager approves first, and the request then routes
to the department head. Both approvals must be recorded before the absence begins.

Note that the 10-consecutive-day threshold for second approval and the 20-business-day
notice threshold for requests of 6 or more days are separate rules that both apply. A
request of 10 or more consecutive days requires 20 business days of notice **and** two
approvals.

### 3.3 What approvers assess

An approver should confirm, in order:

1. That the requested dates are on or after day 91 of employment, per §2.4.
2. That the notice period for the requested length has been met, per §3.1.
3. That the employee's balance will be sufficient as at the requested dates, per §2.3.
4. That team coverage is workable for the requested dates.

Only the fourth of these is a judgment call. The first three are determined by this
policy, and an approver has no discretion to waive them except through the exception
route in §3.5.

### 3.4 When the approval chain terminates

Some employees have no recorded manager — most commonly the CEO, whose manager field is
empty by design. Where an employee has no direct manager, the PTO request routes to
People Operations, which records the approval on behalf of the approval chain. Where
such a request is 10 or more consecutive days and there is likewise no department head
above the employee, the second approval is also recorded by People Operations.

A missing manager is not a reason to reject a request and is not an error to be
escalated as a data problem. It is an expected condition with a defined route.

### 3.5 Exceptions

Exceptions to the notice periods in §3.1 are granted sparingly and require the approval
of both the direct manager and the department head, regardless of the length of the
request. An exception request should state why the absence could not have been
foreseen. Exceptions are not available for the probation restriction in §2.4 or for the
balance rule in §2.3; neither can be waived.

Unexpected illness is not an exception to a PTO notice period, because it is not PTO.
Illness is covered by sick leave under §6, which has no notice requirement.

### 3.6 Changing or cancelling an approved request

An employee may cancel or shorten an approved PTO request at any time before the first
day of absence, and the unused days return to the balance. Extending an approved
request is treated as a new request for the additional days, and the notice tier for
the new request is set by the total length of the extended block.

## 4. Carryover and Expiration

Unused PTO carries over from one calendar year into the next, up to a cap of **5 days**.
Carried-over days **expire on March 31** of the new year if they have not been used by
that date.

Northwind's fiscal and leave year runs from January 1 to December 31. At the close of
the leave year, any balance above 5 days is forfeited, and up to 5 days are credited
into the new year as carryover. Carryover is not paid out in cash and cannot be
converted into any other benefit.

### 4.1 How carryover is applied

Carried-over days are used before newly accrued days. An employee who takes PTO between
January 1 and March 31 draws down the carryover balance first, which reduces the amount
at risk of expiring. This ordering is applied automatically in the HR portal and is not
something the employee needs to elect.

### 4.2 Expiration is not extended

Carryover that is unused on March 31 expires. It is not paid out, it is not restored,
and it does not roll into the following year's carryover. Expiration applies even where
an employee submitted a request in good time and the request was declined, and even
where business demands made it difficult to take the days.

Employees who are holding carryover should plan those days early in the year and should
account for the notice periods in §3.1 when doing so. A 6-day block taken in late March
requires 20 business days of notice, which means the request has to be submitted well
before the expiry date. Managers are expected to look at team carryover balances in the
first weeks of the year and to protect time for employees who are carrying days
forward.

### 4.3 Carryover and other leave types

Only PTO carries over. Sick leave does not carry over under any circumstances, as
stated in §6. Bereavement, parental, and unpaid leave are entitlements rather than
accrued banks and so have nothing to carry over.

### 4.4 Carryover and part-time employees

Part-time employees accrue PTO pro-rated by scheduled hours under §2.1, but the
carryover cap is not pro-rated. The cap is 5 days for every employee, and the March 31
expiry date is the same for every employee.

## 5. Payout on Termination

Northwind **pays out unused PTO on termination of employment, capped at 10 days**. The
payout applies whether the employee resigns or the employment is ended by Northwind,
and it is made at the employee's rate of pay through the final payroll cycle following
the last day of employment.

### 5.1 How the payout is calculated

The payout is the employee's PTO balance as at the last day of employment, up to a
maximum of 10 days. A balance above 10 days is paid at the 10-day cap and the remainder
is forfeited. A balance below the cap is paid in full.

Carryover from the prior year that is still unexpired forms part of the balance and is
included in the payout, subject to the same 10-day cap. Carryover that expired on
March 31 is gone and is not restored by termination.

Accrual continues through the last day of employment, so an employee who leaves partway
through a month accrues for that month on the normal monthly basis described in §2.1.
Part-time employees are paid out on the same 10-day cap; the cap is not pro-rated.

### 5.2 What is not paid out

Sick leave is never paid out on termination. It is an entitlement to be absent when
unwell, not a bank of value. The same is true of bereavement leave and of any
unused portion of a parental leave entitlement.

PTO that is above the 10-day payout cap is not paid out, and it cannot be taken as
leave during a notice period solely in order to avoid the cap without the normal
approvals in §3 being applied. Notice-period absences remain subject to manager
approval.

### 5.3 Employees who leave during probation

An employee whose employment ends during probation has accrued PTO
under §2.2 even though, under §2.4, they were never able to take it. That accrued
balance is paid out under this section, subject to the same 10-day cap.

### 5.4 Negative balances

Because PTO cannot be taken in advance of accrual under §2.3, a departing employee
cannot hold a negative balance, and there is nothing to recover from final pay in
respect of PTO.

## 6. Sick Leave

Sick leave is **separate from PTO**. Employees receive **8 days of sick leave per
year**. Sick leave **does not accrue** — the full annual entitlement is available from
the start of the leave year — and it **does not carry over** into the following year.

### 6.1 What sick leave covers

Sick leave may be used for the employee's own illness or injury, for medical and dental
appointments, for recovery from a procedure, and for the care of a family member in the
same circumstances. It may also be used for mental health needs on the same basis as
physical health needs.

### 6.2 Notice and approval

Sick leave has **no advance notice requirement**. An employee who is unwell should
notify their direct manager as early as practicable on the day, by whatever channel the
team normally uses, and record the absence in the HR portal when able to do so. A
manager does not approve or decline sick leave in the way they approve PTO; the
manager's role is to acknowledge the absence and arrange coverage.

Foreseeable sick leave — a scheduled procedure or a planned appointment — should be
recorded in the HR portal in advance so that the team can plan, but it remains sick
leave and is not converted into PTO because it was foreseeable.

### 6.3 Sick leave and the PTO bank

Sick leave is drawn from the sick bank, not the PTO bank, and an employee who exhausts
the 8-day sick entitlement does not automatically draw on PTO. An employee who is
unwell beyond the sick entitlement may request PTO for the additional days, subject to
the notice rules in §3 and any exception granted under §3.5, or may discuss a longer
absence with People Operations under §9.

An employee who falls ill during a period of approved PTO may ask their manager to
reclassify the affected days as sick leave, which returns those days to the PTO
balance. The request should be made during or promptly after the absence.

### 6.4 New employees and part-time employees

The 8-day sick entitlement is available from the first day of employment. The
probation restriction in §2.4 applies to PTO only and does not restrict sick leave; an
employee who is unwell during probation uses sick leave in the normal way.

Sick leave is not pro-rated by scheduled hours. Part-time employees receive the same
8 days per year as full-time employees.

### 6.5 Sick leave and termination

Unused sick leave is not paid out on termination, as stated in §5.2, and does not carry
into any subsequent period of employment.

## 7. Bereavement Leave

Northwind provides paid bereavement leave separately from PTO and separately from sick
leave. The entitlement is **5 days for the death of an immediate family member** and
**2 days for the death of an extended family member**.

### 7.1 Immediate and extended family

For the purposes of this section, immediate family means a spouse or domestic partner,
a child, a parent, a sibling, and the equivalent relationships of a spouse or domestic
partner, as well as any person who stood in one of those roles in the employee's life.
Extended family means grandparents, grandchildren, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, and
cousins.

Northwind recognises that families are not uniform and that the person an employee has
lost may not map neatly onto a list. Where a relationship falls outside the definitions
above but was, in substance, an immediate family relationship, the direct manager may
apply the 5-day entitlement with People Operations' agreement. This is the one place in
this policy where an approver is expected to read the entitlement generously.

### 7.2 Taking bereavement leave

Bereavement leave has no advance notice requirement and no approval threshold. The
employee notifies their direct manager, takes the days, and records the absence in the
HR portal when they are able to. The days do not need to be taken consecutively, and
they do not need to be taken immediately; an employee travelling for a memorial some
weeks later may hold the days for that purpose.

### 7.3 Extending time away

An employee who needs more time than the bereavement entitlement provides may use PTO,
subject to §3, or may request unpaid leave under §9. Managers should raise these
options with the employee rather than waiting to be asked. Bereavement leave does not
accrue, does not carry over, and is not paid out on termination.

## 8. Parental Leave

Northwind provides paid parental leave to employees who have completed **12 months of
tenure** at the point the leave begins. The entitlement is **16 weeks for a primary
caregiver** and **6 weeks for a secondary caregiver**.

### 8.1 Eligibility

Eligibility depends on tenure at the start of the leave, not at the date of the request
or the date of the birth or placement. An employee who has not yet reached 12 months of
tenure may still submit a request, and the leave may be approved to begin on or after
the date the 12-month threshold is met.

Parental leave is available for the birth of a child, for adoption, and for the
placement of a child in foster care. It is available to any eligible employee
regardless of gender, and the primary and secondary designations refer to caregiving
role rather than to any other characteristic.

### 8.2 Primary and secondary caregiver

The primary caregiver is the parent with principal day-to-day responsibility for the
child during the leave period. The secondary caregiver is the other parent. An
employee designates their caregiver role when requesting the leave, and the designation
is recorded by People Operations.

Where both parents work at Northwind, one may be designated primary and the other
secondary. The designations cannot both be primary for the same child.

### 8.3 Requesting parental leave

Parental leave is requested through People Operations rather than through the ordinary
PTO flow in §3, because it requires an entitlement check against tenure and a coverage
plan across the length of the absence. The direct manager is informed and is
responsible for the coverage plan; the manager does not approve or decline the
entitlement itself, which follows from eligibility.

Employees should notify People Operations as early as they reasonably can so that
coverage can be planned. The weeks are stated in calendar weeks and are ordinarily
taken as a single continuous block beginning on or after the birth, adoption, or
placement.

### 8.4 Pay, benefits, and accrual during parental leave

Parental leave is paid leave. PTO continues to accrue during parental leave at the
employee's normal rate under §2.5, and benefits elections continue unchanged; see
BEN-01 for the treatment of contributions and coverage during a paid leave.

Parental leave is separate from the PTO bank and does not draw it down. An employee
may attach PTO to the start or end of a parental leave block, subject to the notice and
approval rules in §3.

### 8.5 Returning to work

An employee returning from parental leave returns to their role. An employee who wishes
to change their working pattern on return — for example, moving from full-time to
part-time hours — should raise it with their manager and People Operations before the
leave ends, since a change in scheduled hours affects PTO accrual under §2.1 and may
affect benefits eligibility under BEN-01.

## 9. Unpaid Leave of Absence

Unpaid leave is available where an employee needs an extended absence that is not
covered by PTO, sick leave, bereavement leave, or parental leave. Unpaid leave requires
the approval of **both HR and the department head**, and may last a **maximum of 90
days**. At Northwind the HR function is People Operations, and the two terms mean the
same approver here.

### 9.1 When unpaid leave is appropriate

Unpaid leave is intended for circumstances such as a serious personal or family medical
situation that outlasts the sick entitlement, a family obligation requiring extended
absence, or a compelling personal circumstance that cannot be accommodated within the
paid leave types in this policy. It is not a mechanism for extending a vacation beyond
the available PTO balance, and it is not available as a substitute for a PTO request
that failed the notice rules in §3.1.

### 9.2 Requesting unpaid leave

A request is submitted to People Operations with the proposed start date, the proposed
length, and the reason at whatever level of detail the employee is comfortable
providing. People Operations reviews the request against this policy, and the
department head reviews the coverage and business impact. Both approvals must be
recorded before the leave begins. The direct manager is consulted as part of the
department head's review but is not a separate approver for unpaid leave.

Employees should exhaust available PTO before beginning unpaid leave where the absence
is one that PTO could cover, but they are not required to reduce their PTO balance to
zero as a condition of approval.

### 9.3 The 90-day maximum

The maximum duration of an unpaid leave of absence is 90 days. A request cannot be
approved for a longer period, and successive unpaid leaves cannot be used to extend an
absence beyond the maximum. Where an employee's circumstances require a longer absence,
the employee and People Operations should discuss the options available, which may
include a change in employment status.

### 9.4 Accrual, benefits, and return

PTO does not accrue during unpaid leave, as stated in §2.5, and accrual resumes on the
first day back at work. The treatment of benefits coverage and contributions during an
unpaid leave is set out in BEN-01. An employee on unpaid leave remains employed and
remains bound by all Northwind policies for the duration of the leave.

An employee who does not return on the agreed date, and who has not agreed a revised
date with People Operations, is treated as having ended their employment, and the PTO
payout rules in §5 apply.

## 10. Interaction with Probation, Remote Work, and Benefits

This section exists because the most common leave questions at Northwind are not
answered by a single rule. They sit at the boundary between this policy and another
one.

### 10.1 Probation (ONB-01)

The length of the probation period and the schedule of probation reviews are defined in
ONB-01. This policy adds one rule on top of them: during probation, PTO accrues from the
first day of employment under §2.2 but cannot be taken before day 91 under §2.4.

An employee in probation who asks about PTO should be told both halves of this: the
balance is real and is growing, and it is not yet available to take. Where probation has
been extended under the terms set out in ONB-01, PTO still becomes usable on day 91 of
employment; the usability date does not move with the extension.

Sick leave, bereavement leave, and the 8-day sick entitlement are not restricted by
probation. Parental leave has its own 12-month tenure requirement under §8, which is
independent of probation and longer than it.

### 10.2 Remote and hybrid work (REM-01)

Working remotely is not leave. An employee working from another location is working and
does not submit a PTO request for those days. REM-01 governs where an employee may work
from, including the rules for out-of-state and international work, and TAX-01 governs
the tax consequences of working from another jurisdiction.

The distinction matters when the two are combined. An employee who wants to take PTO in
another country and then work from that country for the rest of the trip is making two
separate requests: a PTO request under §3 of this policy for the days off, and an
international work request under REM-01 for the working days. The PTO approval does not
imply approval to work from that location, and REM-01's approval requirements, country
restrictions, and minimum notice apply to the working portion in full. Neither request
can be used to obtain what the other would refuse.

Employees should also note that REM-01's core overlap hours apply to working days and
not to days of approved leave. An employee on approved PTO is not expected to be
available during core hours.

### 10.3 Benefits (BEN-01)

Leave accrual and benefits eligibility are governed by different rules and should not
be confused. PTO accrual is pro-rated by scheduled hours for part-time employees under
§2.1, and there is no minimum number of hours an employee must be scheduled in order to
accrue PTO at all. Benefits eligibility is a separate question with its own
scheduled-hours threshold, which is defined in BEN-01 and not in this policy.

A part-time employee therefore accrues pro-rated PTO under §2.1 and receives the full
8-day sick entitlement under §6.4 regardless of how many hours they are scheduled.
Whether that same employee is eligible for medical, dental, or vision coverage is not
answered here — it is determined solely by BEN-01. These are two different questions
with two different sources, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.

### 10.4 Contractors

Contractors accrue nothing under this policy and are not eligible for any leave type
described in it, nor for any benefit under BEN-01. A contractor asking about PTO should
be routed to the engaging manager and People Operations rather than being given a
balance-based answer.

### 10.5 Ireland entity

Employees of Northwind Systems Ltd. in Dublin are covered by the Irish statutory leave
schedule rather than by the accrual, carryover, and payout rules in this policy, and by
the separate Irish benefits schedule described in BEN-01 §9. The notice-period
expectations in §3.1 and the approval structure in §3.2 are applied in Dublin as a
matter of practice so that coverage planning works consistently across entities.

## 11. Worked Examples

These examples show how the rules in this policy combine. Each one states the facts, the
rules that apply, and the outcome.

### 11.1 Sufficient balance, insufficient notice

A full-time employee with a PTO balance of 9.0 days asks on a Monday for three days off
the following week. The balance is more than sufficient. The request is for 3 days,
which falls in the 3–5 day tier and requires 10 business days of notice under §3.1. The
following week is well short of 10 business days away.

**Outcome:** declined on notice. The employee may move the dates so that at least 10
business days of notice is given, reduce the request to 1–2 days, which requires only 3
business days of notice, or ask the manager to seek an exception under §3.5 on the
grounds that the need could not have been foreseen. The balance is not the issue and
should not be given as the reason.

### 11.2 Inside the probation window

An employee who started recently has a PTO balance of 6.5 days and asks for two days off
next month, on dates that still fall inside the probation period. The notice
period for a 1–2 day request is 3 business days and is comfortably met. The balance is
sufficient.

**Outcome:** declined under §2.4. PTO cannot be taken before day 91 of employment. The
employee may resubmit for dates on or after day 91. If the absence is due to illness,
it is sick leave under §6 and is unaffected by probation.

### 11.3 Insufficient balance

A full-time employee with a PTO balance of 2.5 days asks for a 5-day block three months
out. The notice period for a 3–5 day request is 10 business days and is met. The
employee will accrue 1.67 days per month between now and then under §2.1.

**Outcome:** the balance must be assessed as at the requested dates, not today, under
§2.3. The manager should check the projected balance for those dates in the HR portal.
If the projected balance is still short of 5 days, the request is declined on balance,
and the employee may reduce the number of days, move the dates later, or request unpaid
leave under §9.

### 11.4 A long block requiring two approvals

A full-time employee requests a block of 10 consecutive days. Two rules apply. The
request is 6 or more days, so it requires 20 business days of notice under §3.1. It is
also 10 or more consecutive days, so it requires the department head's approval in
addition to the direct manager's under §3.2.

**Outcome:** approved only if the notice period is met and both approvals are recorded
before the absence begins. A direct manager approval alone is not sufficient.

### 11.5 Carryover about to expire

An employee begins the year with 5 days of carryover and wants to use all of it before
it expires on March 31. A 5-day request falls in the 3–5 day tier and requires 10
business days of notice.

**Outcome:** the request must be submitted at least 10 business days before the first
day of absence, and the absence must conclude on or before March 31 for the carried-over
days to be used. Under §4.1, PTO taken in this window draws down carryover first.
Carryover that is unused on March 31 expires under §4.2 and is not paid out.

### 11.6 An employee with no manager

An employee whose HR record has no manager submits a PTO request.

**Outcome:** the request routes to People Operations under §3.4, which records the
approval. The absence of a manager is expected for this record and is not an error.

### 11.7 PTO combined with work from another country

An employee asks to take PTO abroad and then work from the same country for several
additional weeks.

**Outcome:** two separate requests under §10.2. The PTO days are assessed under §3 of
this policy. The working days are assessed entirely under REM-01, including its country
restrictions, duration ceilings, approval requirements, and minimum notice, with tax
consequences under TAX-01. Approving the PTO does not approve the working portion, and
a working portion that REM-01 prohibits cannot be reclassified as PTO to get around the
prohibition.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

**Does PTO accrue while I am on probation?**
Yes. Accrual starts on the first day of employment under §2.2. The days cannot be taken
before day 91 under §2.4.

**Is sick leave part of my PTO balance?**
No. Sick leave is separate, is 8 days per year, does not accrue, and does not carry
over. See §6.

**How much PTO can I carry into next year?**
Up to 5 days, and it expires on March 31. See §4.

**What happens to my PTO if I leave?**
Unused PTO is paid out, capped at 10 days. Sick leave is not paid out. See §5.

**How much notice do I need to give?**
3 business days for 1–2 days, 10 business days for 3–5 days, and 20 business days for 6
or more days. See §3.1.

**Who approves my request?**
Your direct manager. Requests of 10 or more consecutive days also require the department
head. See §3.2.

**I have plenty of PTO — why was my request declined?**
Most likely on notice rather than balance. The two tests are independent. See §3.1 and
§11.1.

**Can I take PTO I have not yet accrued?**
No. Balances cannot go negative. See §2.3.

**I am part-time. Do I get less PTO?**
You accrue pro-rated by your scheduled hours under §2.1. The carryover cap, the payout
cap, and the 8-day sick entitlement are not pro-rated.

**I am a contractor. What is my PTO entitlement?**
Contractors do not accrue PTO or any other leave under this policy. See §10.4.

**Am I eligible for parental leave?**
If you have 12 months of tenure when the leave begins. The entitlement is 16 weeks
primary or 6 weeks secondary. See §8.

**Can I take unpaid leave instead of PTO?**
Unpaid leave is for circumstances that the paid leave types do not cover, needs HR
(People Operations) and department head approval, and is capped at 90 days. It is not a way
around a declined PTO request. See §9.

**Does working from another location count as leave?**
No. That is a remote work question under REM-01, not a leave question. See §10.2.

## 13. Roles, Responsibilities, and Document Control

### 13.1 Employees

Employees are responsible for submitting requests in the HR portal with the notice
required by §3.1, for checking their balance before requesting, for recording sick and
bereavement absences when able, and for planning carryover early enough in the year to
use it before it expires under §4.

### 13.2 Managers

Direct managers are responsible for approving or declining PTO requests in the HR portal
in good time, for applying the tests in §3.3 in order, for stating the actual reason
when declining so the employee can act on it, for arranging coverage during absences,
and for monitoring team carryover balances in the first weeks of the year.

Managers should not approve PTO for dates on or before day 90 of an employee's
employment, should not treat verbal agreement as approval, and should route requests of
10 or more consecutive days onward for department head approval.

### 13.3 Department heads

Department heads provide the second approval for requests of 10 or more consecutive
days under §3.2, approve exceptions to notice periods under §3.5 alongside the direct
manager, and approve unpaid leave alongside People Operations under §9.

### 13.4 People Operations

People Operations owns this policy, maintains balances and entitlements in the HR
portal, administers parental leave under §8, approves unpaid leave under §9, records
approvals where the approval chain terminates under §3.4, and applies the year-end
carryover and March 31 expiration process under §4.

### 13.5 Raising a concern

An employee who believes a leave request was handled unfairly should raise it with their
direct manager in the first instance, and may raise it with their HR Business Partner or
through the anonymous ethics line. The reporting channels, acknowledgment timelines, and
anti-retaliation protections are set out in CON-01.

### 13.6 Out of scope

This policy covers PTO, sick leave, bereavement leave, parental leave, and unpaid leave
of absence. It does not cover any other form of extended time away from work, and no
entitlement should be inferred from silence. Where an employee asks about a form of
leave or a benefit that is not described in this policy or in another Northwind policy
document, the correct answer is that the policy library does not cover it and the
question should be referred to People Operations.

### 13.7 Version and review

This is version v4.2, effective 2026-01-01. It supersedes all prior versions of the
Northwind PTO and Leave Policy. The policy is owned by People Operations and is reviewed
annually. A consolidated view of every approval threshold in this policy, alongside the
thresholds from the other Northwind policy documents, is maintained in APR-01.
